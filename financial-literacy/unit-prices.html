Compare Prices and Unit Rates

A lower sticker price does not always mean better value. A unit price compares the cost for one standard unit. A fair comparison also considers quality, suitability, delivery charges and whether the item is needed.

Learning time: 15–20 minutes · Beginner level · Free study resource

What you will learn

  • Calculate unit price from total price and quantity.
  • Compare different package sizes.
  • Include extra costs in a fair comparison.
  • Explain why the lowest price is not always best value.

1. Calculate a unit price

Unit price = total price ÷ number of units. Make sure units match before comparing, such as cost per item or per kilogram.

A pack of 4 notebooks costs 12 units: 12 ÷ 4 = 3 units per notebook. A pack of 6 costs 15 units: 15 ÷ 6 = 2.5 units per notebook.

2. Compare like with like

Compare the same quantity unit and check that products are sufficiently similar. A larger pack may have a lower unit price but can be wasteful if not all of it is used.

Check delivery fees, taxes where applicable, quality, durability and return conditions. A headline discount can hide extra costs.

3. Choose based on value

Value means how well an option meets a need for its total cost. The cheapest option may be unsuitable or poor quality. Explain the evidence behind a choice.

For a classroom comparison, list price, quantity, unit price and extra charges. Then explain which option suits the stated need.

Common mistakes to avoid

Dividing quantity by price instead of price by quantity.
Comparing grams with kilograms without conversion.
Ignoring fees, quality or waste when choosing.

Practice exercises

1. A pack of 5 pens costs 10 units. Find the price per pen.

2. A 2 kg bag costs 6 units. Find the cost per kg.

3. Why might the lowest unit price not be best?
