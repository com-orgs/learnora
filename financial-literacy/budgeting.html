Needs, Wants and Simple Budgets

A budget is a plan for using money over a chosen period. It helps compare money available with planned spending and saving. Practise with fictional amounts; never share private family financial information.

Learning time: 15–20 minutes · Beginner level · Free study resource

What you will learn

  • Distinguish needs from wants using context.
  • List available money and planned uses.
  • Calculate a budget balance.
  • Adjust a plan when spending exceeds available money.

1. Needs and wants

A need is important for health, safety or essential participation, such as basic food or necessary transport. A want is desirable but not essential. Context can matter.

A notebook may be needed for a class task, while an extra decorative notebook may be a want. Explain the reason rather than assuming every item always fits one category.

2. Make a simple budget

Write the amount available, then list planned spending and savings. Add planned amounts and compare the total with the available amount.

Example: 30 units available; 12 for transport, 8 for supplies and 5 for saving. Planned uses total 25 units, leaving 5 units unallocated. These are fictional classroom units.

3. Adjust and review

If planned spending exceeds the amount available, review flexible wants, compare alternatives or revise the plan. A budget is a guide and may need to change when prices or plans change.

Keep essential needs and safety in mind. A classroom example does not represent every family's circumstances.

Common mistakes to avoid

Forgetting to include savings in the total.
Treating every want as bad rather than prioritising.
Assuming a classroom budget represents all families.

Practice exercises

1. Classify essential school transport and an optional game, explaining context.

2. A student has 40 units and plans 15, 10 and 8. Find the balance.

3. What can change if a plan costs too much?

Knowledge check: five questions

Choose one answer for each question. Feedback explains every answer.

1. What is a budget?
2. Which may be a need for a school day?
3. 30 units minus plans of 10, 8 and 5 leaves?
4. What if planned spending exceeds available money?
5. Are classroom budget examples personal advice?
