Savings Goals and Simple Interest

Saving means setting aside part of the money available for a future goal or unexpected cost. A clear goal includes a target amount and a time frame. Interest is one way savings may grow, depending on account terms.

Learning time: 15–20 minutes · Beginner level · Free study resource

What you will learn

  • Set a measurable savings goal.
  • Calculate contributions toward a target.
  • Use the simple-interest formula.
  • Recognise that real account terms and fees vary.

1. Turn a wish into a goal

A useful goal states what is being saved for, the target amount and a time frame. Divide the amount still needed by the number of saving periods to estimate a regular contribution.

Target 60 units; 15 already saved; 9 weeks remain. The remaining 45 units require 5 units each week if contributions are equal.

2. Understand simple interest

Simple interest is calculated on the original principal. Formula: I = P × r × t, where P is principal, r is annual rate as a decimal and t is time in years.

If P = 100 units, r = 5% = 0.05 and t = 2 years, then I = 100 × 0.05 × 2 = 10 units. This is a simplified classroom example.

3. Check real-world terms

Savings products may have different rates, compounding rules, minimum balances, access limits, taxes and fees. An advertised rate alone does not describe the whole product.

For a real decision, involve a parent or guardian and review official account terms. This lesson is general education, not a product recommendation.

Common mistakes to avoid

Using 5 instead of 0.05 for a 5% decimal rate.
Mixing months and years without converting consistently.
Assuming all accounts use simple interest or have no fees.

Practice exercises

1. A target is 80 units and 20 are saved. Find the remainder.

2. Calculate simple interest on 200 units at 3% for 2 years.

3. Name two account conditions that can affect savings.

Knowledge check: five questions

Choose one answer for each question. Feedback explains every answer.

1. What makes a savings goal measurable?
2. A 60-unit goal has 15 saved. How much remains?
3. 5% as a decimal is?
4. Interest on 100 at 5% for 2 years?
5. What should be checked before a real savings decision?
