# Learnora Editorial Content Quality Standard

Status: Internal working standard for Phase 2
Applies to: Educational lessons, learning paths, worksheets, quizzes and curriculum guides
Purpose: Ensure each page teaches accurately and usefully; this is an internal QA rubric, not a Google ranking score or a Google-issued certification.

## Non-negotiable rules
- Never publish a factual or mathematical error knowingly. Any critical accuracy error fails the page regardless of its numeric score.
- Do not add paragraphs merely to increase word count.
- Do not repeat keywords unnaturally or create near-duplicate pages to target minor keyword variations.
- Do not invent authors, reviewers, qualifications, endorsements, learner testimonials, usage statistics or first-hand experience.
- Do not describe a lesson as officially curriculum-aligned unless a documented, checked mapping supports that exact claim.
- AI-assisted drafting is allowed as a production aid, but each claim, example, calculation, answer key and explanation must be checked by an editor. AI output is not evidence.
- Attribute external ideas, quotations, data and specialist claims with relevant, working sources. Prefer primary sources and authoritative educational references.
- Clearly distinguish established facts, worked examples, assumptions, illustrative fictional data and advice.
- Make the learning outcome clear and match the actual lesson content.

## Internal 100-point page-quality rubric
| Dimension | Points | Pass criteria |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Accuracy and source quality | 25 | Definitions, facts, calculations, diagrams, quiz keys and worksheet answers are correct; important factual claims have suitable sources. |
| Teaching clarity and progression | 20 | Prerequisites are considered; concepts are explained in plain language; the lesson moves from simple to more demanding ideas. |
| Worked examples and reasoning | 15 | Examples show steps and explain why the method works, not only the final answer. |
| Practice and feedback | 15 | Exercises test the stated objectives; answers include enough reasoning to diagnose misconceptions; quizzes have checked mappings and useful feedback. |
| Original educational value | 10 | Includes independently developed explanations, examples, comparisons, problem contexts or teaching choices; not a superficial paraphrase of sources. |
| Grade and audience suitability | 5 | Vocabulary, complexity, examples and assumed knowledge suit the stated learner level; curriculum scope is represented honestly. |
| Accessibility and usability | 5 | Semantic headings, readable layout, accessible controls/labels, useful text alternatives and printable material where promised. |
| Transparency and maintenance | 5 | Sources work; limitations and curriculum scope are clear; correction/contact route is available; update-sensitive material is dated where appropriate. |

## Release gates
- 95–100: Ready after final technical and content verification.
- 90–94: Minor revisions required before release.
- Below 90: Revise and re-audit.
- Any critical factual error, wrong answer key, misleading curriculum claim, fabricated trust signal or broken core learning interaction: automatic fail until corrected.
- A page's score is an internal editorial assessment only. It does not predict Google rankings, guarantee indexing, or represent a Google score.

## Required lesson structure (adapt where the subject needs a different format)
1. Clear title, audience/grade and honest scope.
2. Specific learning objectives.
3. Short explanation of the central concept and why it matters.
4. Definitions of new terms.
5. Worked examples with steps and reasoning.
6. At least one meaningful misconception or comparison where relevant.
7. Independent practice that tests the objectives.
8. Correct answer key with explanations or working.
9. Quiz feedback that teaches, including why wrong options are wrong where useful.
10. Worksheet that is genuinely printable and has a checked answer key.
11. Summary and sensible next lesson links.
12. Sources/further reading with working links where appropriate.

This is a guide, not a mechanical template. Do not force irrelevant sections into a lesson.

## Subject-specific checks
- Mathematics: recalculate every worked example, exercise, quiz answer and worksheet key independently; check units, notation, rounding, equivalent forms and edge cases.
- Science: distinguish model from observation; check terminology and causal explanations against authoritative sources; avoid oversimplifications that become false.
- English: validate grammar examples, punctuation, interpretation and answer explanations; acknowledge legitimate variation where relevant.
- Computer science: test code/pseudocode mentally or in a safe test environment; check version assumptions, outputs and security implications.
- AI literacy: separate established capability from uncertainty; use current primary documentation for fast-changing claims; teach verification, not blind trust.
- Financial literacy: use transparent fictional examples where suitable; distinguish educational arithmetic from personal financial advice; verify percentages, interest and units.

## Page QA record
For each page record:
- URL and source file
- learner audience and stated objectives
- score by each rubric dimension
- factual/answer-key checks completed
- source links checked and their relevance
- originality/value-added notes
- accessibility and interactive checks
- changes made and commit
- independent recheck result
- unresolved issues and owner

## AI Search and discoverability
Write content so that a learner can understand it without needing to click elsewhere. Use descriptive headings, concise definitions, complete answer explanations and explicit context. Use structured data only when it accurately describes visible page content and follows the relevant search engine rules. Do not write artificial FAQ blocks, schema or text solely to manipulate snippets or AI answers.
