Grade 6–8 Maths Study Plan and Skills Checklist

A practical four-week plan to review middle-school mathematics, identify gaps and practise with purpose. It focuses on transferable skills rather than claiming to reproduce one national syllabus.

Important: Grade and year expectations differ between countries, states, provinces and schools. Use this plan alongside current classwork and the official local curriculum. It is supplementary revision, not a formal assessment or guarantee of curriculum coverage.

How to use this plan

  1. Study for 25–35 minutes, four or five days each week.
  2. Begin with a retrieval check: write what you remember before looking at notes.
  3. Follow one worked example, then solve a new problem without copying.
  4. Keep an error log: note the mistaken step, the correction and a date to retry.
  5. Revisit difficult skills after two days and again the following week.

Skills checklist

Number and proportional reasoning

  • Compare fractions, decimals and percentages.
  • Calculate with rational numbers at the level being studied.
  • Use ratios, rates and unit rates in context.
  • Estimate and check whether an answer is reasonable.

Algebra and functions

  • Identify variables, terms, coefficients and constants.
  • Write and simplify expressions from words or patterns.
  • Solve equations and check by substitution.
  • Read tables and graphs and describe relationships.

Geometry and measurement

  • Use angle facts and properties of common shapes.
  • Calculate perimeter, area and volume with correct units.
  • Recognise transformations and compare similar figures.
  • Use right-triangle reasoning where it is part of the course.

Statistics and probability

  • Read tables and graph scales accurately.
  • Calculate and interpret mean, median, mode or range when appropriate.
  • Describe patterns, outliers and data limitations.
  • Use sample spaces and simple probability models.

Worked examples

Fraction of a quantity

Find three-fifths of 40. Divide 40 by 5 to get 8, then multiply by 3: 24. This is sensible because three-fifths is less than one whole.

Solving an equation

Solve 3x + 5 = 20. Subtract 5: 3x = 15. Divide by 3: x = 5. Check: 3(5) + 5 = 20.

Area and units

A rectangle is 8 cm by 5 cm. Area = 8 × 5 = 40 cm². The square unit matters because area measures a surface.

Independent practice

Try these without looking at the answer key. Show working for questions 4–8.

  1. Write 0.35 as a fraction in simplest form.
  2. Find 15% of 80.
  3. A recipe uses 2 cups of rice for 5 people. How many cups for 10 people at the same rate?
  4. Simplify 4a + 3a − 2.
  5. Solve 2x − 7 = 13.
  6. A rectangle is 9 m long and 4 m wide. Find its perimeter and area.
  7. Two angles on a straight line are x° and 125°. Find x.
  8. A bag has 3 red, 2 blue and 5 green counters. Find the probability of choosing blue.
  9. For 4, 6, 6, 8 and 11, find the mean and median.
  10. Why does an upward trend in a scatter plot not prove that one variable causes the other?

Answer key and reasoning

Show answers and explanations
  1. 35/100 = 7/20.
  2. 0.15 × 80 = 12.
  3. Double the recipe: 4 cups.
  4. Combine like terms: 7a − 2.
  5. 2x = 20, so x = 10.
  6. Perimeter = 2(9 + 4) = 26 m; area = 9 × 4 = 36 m².
  7. x = 180° − 125° = 55°.
  8. 2 of 10 counters are blue: 1/5.
  9. Mean = 35 ÷ 5 = 7; median = 6.
  10. Correlation describes association, not proof of cause. Other variables or chance may explain the pattern.

Choose lessons for extra practice

  • Number: fractions, decimals, percentages, rational numbers.
  • Algebra: one-step equations, two-step equations, linear equations, linear functions.
  • Geometry: angles, area, right triangles.
  • Data: graphs, statistics, scatter plots.
