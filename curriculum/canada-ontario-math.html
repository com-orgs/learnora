Canada · Ontario

Ontario Mathematics: Grades 6–8

Canada does not have one single national school curriculum: provinces and territories set their own education programmes. This guide focuses specifically on Ontario and its Mathematics (2020) curriculum for Grades 1–8.

Ontario's mathematics framework

Ontario's current elementary mathematics policy says that Grades 1–8 programmes are based on the Mathematics (2020) expectations. The framework includes mathematical processes and learning across number, algebra, data, spatial sense and financial literacy. Expectations and examples vary by grade, so check the grade-specific official pages.

Use the Ontario Mathematics (2020) curriculum and its Grade 6, Grade 7 and Grade 8 expectations.

Ontario's six areas of study

The Ontario Mathematics (2020) curriculum is organised around six areas. The first area focuses on social-emotional learning skills and mathematical processes; the remaining areas cover Number, Algebra, Data, Spatial Sense and Financial Literacy. The expectations are described by grade, so a Grade 6 activity should be checked against the Grade 6 page rather than assumed to fit Grade 7 or Grade 8.

Number and algebra

Number work develops flexible understanding of fractions, decimals, percentages and operations. Algebra helps learners recognise patterns, represent relationships and solve problems with variables. Related Learnora practice includes decimal place value, percentages, algebraic expressions and linear equations.

Data, spatial sense and financial literacy

Data work supports interpreting and communicating information. Spatial Sense connects geometry, measurement and visual reasoning. Financial Literacy helps learners apply mathematics to money decisions and everyday contexts. Learnora has related data and graphs, area and volume and geometry lessons; financial literacy coverage is still developing and should not be considered complete.

Canada is not one curriculum

Education is a provincial and territorial responsibility. Ontario's Mathematics (2020) policy is not automatically the curriculum for a learner in Alberta, British Columbia, Quebec or another province or territory. For learners outside Ontario, use the local ministry or education department's current programme and treat these Learnora links only as general supplementary practice.

How families can use the guide

Choose the learner's exact grade, open the official Ontario expectations and compare the topic before assigning practice. Ask learners to explain their method, show working and check answers with a second method. A Learnora quiz can help identify a topic to revisit, but it does not replace classroom assessment or the complete Ontario curriculum.

Scope: This page is Ontario-specific, not a Canada-wide curriculum claim. Families in other provinces and territories should consult their own education authority. Learnora is supplementary and is not an Ontario Ministry resource.
