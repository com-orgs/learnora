Canada Grade 6–8 Math Curriculum Guide

Canadian students study within provincial and territorial education systems. There is no single national mathematics curriculum for every school. Learnora provides international Grade 6–8 practice and this guide helps families identify the right local curriculum context.

How school curriculum works in Canada

Responsibility for education rests with Canada's ten provinces and three territories. Each jurisdiction sets its own curriculum, grade expectations and implementation details. Grade labels may look similar across the country, but topic sequencing, terminology and assessment can differ.

Ontario mathematics

Ontario publishes an elementary mathematics curriculum for Grades 1–8, with learning across number, algebra, data, spatial sense, financial literacy and mathematical processes. For Ontario-specific guidance, see Learnora's Ontario mathematics guide and the official provincial Grade 6, Grade 7 and Grade 8 expectations.

Other provinces and territories

Students in British Columbia, Alberta, Saskatchewan, Manitoba, Quebec, New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, Prince Edward Island, Newfoundland and Labrador, Yukon, the Northwest Territories and Nunavut should consult their own education authority. Do not assume that an Ontario curriculum page or another province's grade sequence applies locally.

Use Learnora for topic practice

Learnora's Grade 6, Grade 7 and Grade 8 paths cover commonly taught middle-grade topics, including fractions, decimals, percentages, ratios, integers, algebra, geometry, measurement, data and probability. Use these lessons to review a concept or practise a method, then compare the topic with the learner's current provincial or territorial programme.

Study tips for families

Ask which unit the learner is studying at school. Read the matching explanation, complete a worked example together, then let the learner attempt the questions independently. Check the answer and the method, not only the final number. If a term or method differs from class, follow the teacher's approach and use Learnora as extra practice.

Important: Learnora is an independent supplementary resource. This page explains the Canadian system at a high level; it does not claim province-wide or territory-wide curriculum alignment. The Ontario guide is specific to Ontario.
