England · Key Stage 3

England KS3 Mathematics: Years 7–9

In England, Key Stage 3 (KS3) covers Years 7, 8 and 9. It is not an exact one-to-one equivalent of US or international Grade 6–8 labels. Use this guide to find related Learnora practice while following the learner's school programme.

Key areas in the England maths programme

The Department for Education's KS3 programme develops fluency, mathematical reasoning and problem solving. Topic areas include number; algebra; ratio, proportion and rates of change; geometry and measures; probability; and statistics. Pupils are expected to connect ideas and apply mathematics to increasingly sophisticated problems.

How KS3 maths develops

Key Stage 3 is a three-year stage covering Years 7, 8 and 9 in England. The programme aims for fluency in mathematical fundamentals, reasoning with mathematical language, and solving routine and non-routine problems. It encourages learners to connect topics rather than treat each area as isolated.

Number

KS3 builds confidence with integers, fractions, decimals, percentages, powers, roots and calculations. Learners should choose efficient methods and explain why an answer is reasonable. Related Learnora practice includes integers, fractions and percentages.

Algebra and ratio

Students develop algebraic notation, simplify expressions, substitute values, solve equations and interpret graphs. Ratio, proportion and rates of change connect calculations to recipes, scale, speed, prices and other contexts. See simplifying expressions, two-step equations and ratios and rates.

Geometry, measures and data

Geometry includes properties of shapes, angles, constructions, transformations and measures. Statistics and probability help learners interpret data, compare distributions and reason about chance. Learnora offers related angle and scale drawing, area and volume and probability practice.

Check the right UK curriculum

This guide is specifically about England. Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have different curriculum frameworks and terminology. Even within England, schools may sequence topics differently. Ask the school which topics are being taught and use the Department for Education programme of study as the reference point.
