Australian Curriculum Mathematics: Years 7–8

Australia's national curriculum describes mathematics by year level from Foundation to Year 10. This guide focuses on Years 7 and 8 and points learners to related Learnora Grade 6–8 supplementary practice. Grade and year labels should not be treated as exact equivalents.

Mathematics strands

The Australian Curriculum: Mathematics organises learning through six connected strands: Number, Algebra, Measurement, Space, Statistics and Probability. Learning builds across year levels and develops fluency, reasoning, problem solving and the ability to interpret mathematical situations.

Six connected strands

Australian Curriculum Version 9 presents mathematics through six interrelated strands: Number, Algebra, Measurement, Space, Statistics and Probability. They are connected, so a good learning activity may use number skills while exploring measurement, geometry or data in a real context.

Number and algebra

Year 7 and Year 8 learners extend number skills and use algebra to describe patterns, relationships and unknown values. Related Learnora practice includes integers, fractions, ratios and linear equations.

Measurement and space

Measurement involves choosing and using units, calculating with measures and solving practical problems. Space develops visualisation, geometric properties and spatial reasoning. Use Learnora's metric conversions, area and volume, coordinate geometry and Pythagorean Theorem lessons as extra practice.

Statistics and probability

Statistics supports collecting, representing and interpreting data. Probability helps learners describe and compare the likelihood of events. Related resources include statistics and probability, scatter plots and probability models.

Year levels and local implementation

The Australian Curriculum sets national expectations by year level, but schools and jurisdictions manage implementation and sequencing. Check the current Year 7 or Year 8 content descriptions and the learner's school plan before treating a lesson as a direct match. Grade 6 is not automatically equivalent to Year 6, 7 or 8 across every school system.
