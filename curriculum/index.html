How to use these guides

Start with the country or province where the learner studies, review the official framework, then use Learnora lessons for extra explanation and practice. A topic appearing in a Learnora lesson does not by itself mean the lesson fully covers every curriculum expectation or assessment requirement.

Why curriculum labels differ

Grade and year labels are not interchangeable across countries. England uses Key Stages and school years; Australia uses year levels; Canada sets curricula by province and territory; and US standards adoption and implementation can vary by state and district. Always check the official local curriculum and the learner's school sequence.

What these guides include

Each country guide names the official framework it refers to, explains the local grade or year terminology, and points to Learnora lessons that may provide additional practice. The lesson links are selected by topic, not by a claim that a lesson satisfies every standard, assessment objective or local syllabus requirement.

Which guide should I choose?

  • US learners: start with the Common Core guide if the school or state uses those mathematics standards. State adoption and local pacing should always be checked.
  • England: use the KS3 guide for Years 7–9. Do not treat Grade 6, 7 and 8 as exact Year 7, 8 and 9 equivalents.
  • Canada: the Canadian guide currently focuses on Ontario. Other provinces and territories publish their own curriculum expectations.
  • Australia: use the Australian Curriculum Version 9 guide for Year 7 and Year 8 context. Schools may also publish state, territory or local implementation information.

For parents and teachers

Before assigning a lesson, compare its topic with the learner's current classwork and the official grade-level expectation. Use the explanation first, then ask the learner to solve practice questions without looking at the worked example. Review mistakes together and use the quiz as a quick check for understanding, not as a formal assessment.

Learnora's content is being expanded over time. A linked topic may offer a useful introduction or revision activity while still leaving parts of a local curriculum uncovered. If a topic is missing or a curriculum link changes, contact Learnora with the country, grade or year, and official source page.

Important: Learnora is an independent supplementary learning resource. It is not an official government curriculum provider, examination board or school. The guides are orientation tools, not formal accreditation or a guarantee of curriculum alignment.
