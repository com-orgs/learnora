International Middle-School Maths Worksheets

Four original practice sets for learners studying US Common Core Grades 6–8, England Key Stage 3, Ontario Grades 6–8, and Australian Curriculum Years 7–8. Each set includes questions and a worked answer key.

How to use: Choose the learner's curriculum context, try the questions without notes, show working, then open the answer key to check each method. Use the print button to print the questions and answer keys. Teachers should compare the set with their current class sequence before assigning it.

Important: These are original supplementary revision questions, not official examination papers or a complete test of any national or provincial curriculum. Grade and year labels are not exact equivalents between countries.

1. United States — Common Core Grades 6–8

Focus: ratios and rates, rational numbers, percentages, equations, linear relationships and geometry.

1. A recipe uses 3 cups of flour for 12 muffins. How many cups are needed for 20 muffins at the same rate?
2. Calculate ⅗ ÷ ⅖. Give the answer in simplest form.
3. A jacket costs $64 and is discounted by 25%. What is the sale price?
4. Solve: 4x − 7 = 21.
5. A right triangle has legs of 6 cm and 8 cm. Find its hypotenuse.
6. A taxi fare is $3 plus $2.50 per mile. Write a rule for the cost C after m miles, then find the cost for 4 miles.
Show worked answers — US set

1. 3 ÷ 12 = 0.25 cup per muffin; 20 × 0.25 = 5 cups.

2. ⅗ ÷ ⅖ = ⅗ × ⁵⁄₂ = 15/10 = 3/2 or 1½.

3. 25% of 64 = 0.25 × 64 = 16; 64 − 16 = $48.

4. 4x − 7 = 21; add 7: 4x = 28; divide by 4: x = 7.

5. c² = 6² + 8² = 36 + 64 = 100, so c = 10 cm.

6. C = 3 + 2.5m; for m = 4, C = 3 + 10 = $13.

2. England — Key Stage 3

Focus: number, fractions, percentages, ratio, algebra, angles and probability.

1. Evaluate −7 + 12 − 5.
2. Increase 80 by 15%.
3. Share £54 in the ratio 2:4.
4. Expand and simplify 3(2x + 5) − 4x.
5. Two angles on a straight line are (3x + 10)° and (2x + 20)°. Find x and both angles.
6. A fair six-sided die is rolled once. What is the probability of getting a prime number?
Show worked answers — England KS3 set

1. −7 + 12 = 5; 5 − 5 = 0.

2. 15% of 80 = 12; 80 + 12 = 92.

3. Total parts = 6; one part = 54 ÷ 6 = 9. Shares: 2 × 9 = £18 and 4 × 9 = £36.

4. 6x + 15 − 4x = 2x + 15.

5. Angles on a straight line total 180°: 3x + 10 + 2x + 20 = 180; 5x = 150; x = 30. Angles are 100° and 80°.

6. Prime outcomes are 2, 3 and 5: 3 out of 6, so ½.

3. Canada — Ontario Grades 6–8

Focus: number, algebra, data, spatial sense and financial literacy.

1. A student has $50. They spend ⅕ on a book and $12 on lunch. How much money remains?
2. Evaluate 3.6 ÷ 0.6.
3. Solve: 5n + 8 = 38.
4. A rectangular garden is 8 m long and 5 m wide. Find its area and perimeter.
5. The data set is 4, 6, 6, 8, 11. Find the mean, median and mode.
6. A bank account earns simple interest of 4% per year on $200. How much interest is earned in one year, and what is the total?
Show worked answers — Ontario set

1. Book: ⅕ × $50 = $10. Remaining: 50 − 10 − 12 = $28.

2. 3.6 ÷ 0.6 = 36 ÷ 6 = 6.

3. 5n + 8 = 38; 5n = 30; n = 6.

4. Area = 8 × 5 = 40 m²; perimeter = 2(8 + 5) = 26 m.

5. Mean = 35 ÷ 5 = 7; median = 6; mode = 6.

6. Interest = 0.04 × 200 = $8; total = $208.

4. Australia — Curriculum Years 7–8

Focus: rational numbers, algebra, ratios and rates, measurement, data and probability.

1. Calculate −3.5 + 8.2.
2. A map scale is 1 cm : 5 km. Two towns are 7.4 cm apart on the map. What is the actual distance?
3. Substitute a = 4 and b = 3 into 2a² − b.
4. A rectangular tank is 1.2 m long, 0.5 m wide and 0.4 m high. Find its volume in cubic metres.
5. A bicycle helmet costs $80 before a 10% discount. What is the discounted price?
6. A bag contains 4 red, 3 blue and 1 green counter. What is the probability of selecting a blue counter?
Show worked answers — Australia set

1. −3.5 + 8.2 = 4.7.

2. 7.4 × 5 = 37 km.

3. 2(4²) − 3 = 2 × 16 − 3 = 29.

4. Volume = 1.2 × 0.5 × 0.4 = 0.24 m³.

5. 10% of $80 = $8; $80 − $8 = $72.

6. Total counters = 8; blue counters = 3, so probability = 3/8.
