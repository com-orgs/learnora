Pythagorean Theorem and Right Triangles

A right triangle has one 90° angle. The Pythagorean theorem connects the lengths of its three sides.

Learning time: 20–25 minutes · Level: Developing

Learning goals

  • Identify the hypotenuse and the two shorter sides of a right triangle.
  • Use a² + b² = c² to find a missing side, including rearranging the formula for a shorter side.
  • Use the converse of the theorem to test whether a triangle is right-angled.
  • Apply the theorem to practical distance and measurement problems, with correct units.

1. Identify the hypotenuse

The hypotenuse is the side opposite the right angle. It is always the longest side. The other two sides are often labelled a and b; the hypotenuse is c.

2. The theorem

For a right triangle, a² + b² = c². Use it only when the triangle is right-angled. Square the two shorter sides, add them, then take the square root to find c.

3. Find a shorter side

If the hypotenuse is known, rearrange: a² = c² − b², so a = √(c² − b²). Choose the positive length in ordinary geometry problems.

4. Check for a right triangle

If the longest side is c, check whether a² + b² = c². If the equality holds, the triangle is right-angled.

5. Apply the theorem

A ladder, diagonal across a rectangular floor, or shortest straight-line distance can form a right triangle. Sketch the situation and label the perpendicular sides before calculating.

Use the theorem and verify the triangle

The Pythagorean theorem applies only to right-angled triangles. The hypotenuse is the side opposite the 90° angle and is always the longest side. If the shorter sides are a and b and the hypotenuse is c, then a² + b² = c². To find a shorter side, rearrange the equation: a² = c² − b².

Worked reasoning: A right triangle has shorter sides 9 cm and 12 cm. Then c² = 9² + 12² = 81 + 144 = 225, so c = 15 cm. Check: 9² + 12² = 15².

The converse can test whether a triangle is right-angled: if the square of its longest side equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides, it is a right triangle. For example, 5, 12 and 13 form one because 25 + 144 = 169.

  • Identify the hypotenuse before substituting values.
  • Do not take the square root until after adding or subtracting the squared sides.
  • Use square units during the calculation and ordinary length units for the final answer.

Worked examples

  1. Sides 3 and 4: c = √(3² + 4²) = √25 = 5.
  2. Hypotenuse 13 and one leg 5: other leg = √(13² − 5²) = √144 = 12.
  3. A rectangle 6 cm by 8 cm has diagonal √(36 + 64) = 10 cm.

Independent practice

Try each question before revealing or checking the worked answer.

  1. Find the hypotenuse when legs are 6 and 8. Answer: √(36 + 64) = 10.
  2. Find the hypotenuse for legs 5 and 12. Answer: 13.
  3. Find the missing leg when c = 10 and one leg = 6. Answer: 8.
  4. Is 7, 24, 25 a right-triangle triple? Answer: Yes, 49 + 576 = 625.
  5. A 9 m ladder reaches 8 m up a wall. How far is its foot from the wall? Answer: √(81 − 64) = √17 ≈ 4.12 m.
  6. Find the diagonal of a 9 cm by 12 cm rectangle. Answer: 15 cm.
  7. Do sides 4, 5, 6 form a right triangle? Answer: No, 16 + 25 ≠ 36.
  8. A right triangle has legs 9 and 12. Find its hypotenuse. Answer: 15.
