Volume of Cylinders, Cones and Spheres

Volume measures the space inside a three-dimensional object. These curved solids use formulas involving π and the radius.

Learning time: 20–25 minutes · Level: Developing

Learning goals

  • Identify radius, diameter and perpendicular height in cylinders, cones and spheres.
  • Choose and apply the correct volume formula for each solid.
  • Use consistent units, convert diameter to radius and express answers in cubic units.
  • Compare how changes in dimensions affect volume and round only at the final step.

1. Radius and height

The radius r is the distance from the centre of a circular base to its edge. The height h is the perpendicular distance between bases, or from the apex to the base for a cone.

2. Cylinder

A cylinder has two equal circular bases. Its volume is base area × height: V = πr²h. Keep the radius squared and multiply by the perpendicular height.

3. Cone

A cone has one circular base and a vertex. Its volume is one-third of the matching cylinder: V = ⅓πr²h.

4. Sphere

A sphere is perfectly round in three dimensions. Its volume is V = ⁴⁄₃πr³. Remember that the radius is cubed.

5. Units and rounding

If measurements are in centimetres, volume is in cubic centimetres (cm³). Keep π in the calculation until the final step, then round as requested. Use the same units for all lengths.

Compare solid volumes and use units

Volume measures the space inside a three-dimensional solid and is written in cubic units. A cylinder has volume V = πr²h. A cone with the same base radius and height has one-third of the cylinder's volume: V = ⅓πr²h. A sphere has volume V = ⁴⁄₃πr³. In each formula, r is the radius, not the diameter.

Worked reasoning: A cone has radius 3 cm and height 4 cm. Its volume is ⅓ × π × 3² × 4 = 12π cm³, approximately 37.7 cm³. If the question gives a diameter of 6 cm, first divide by 2 to get the radius 3 cm.

Keep π in the exact answer when appropriate, then round the decimal only at the end. The final unit must be cubic, such as cm³ or m³, because three dimensions are multiplied.

  • Do not use diameter in place of radius.
  • Square the radius for cylinder and cone formulas; cube it for a sphere.
  • Convert all measurements to the same unit before calculating.

Worked examples

  1. Cylinder r = 3 cm, h = 5 cm: V = π(3²)(5) = 45π ≈ 141.4 cm³.
  2. Cone r = 3 cm, h = 5 cm: V = ⅓π(9)(5) = 15π ≈ 47.1 cm³.
  3. Sphere r = 3 cm: V = ⁴⁄₃π(27) = 36π ≈ 113.1 cm³.

Independent practice

Attempt each problem before reviewing the worked answer.

  1. Find cylinder volume for r = 2 cm, h = 7 cm. Answer: 28π ≈ 88.0 cm³.
  2. Find cone volume for r = 3 cm, h = 8 cm. Answer: 24π ≈ 75.4 cm³.
  3. Find sphere volume for r = 2 cm. Answer: 32π/3 ≈ 33.5 cm³.
  4. A cylinder has diameter 10 cm and height 4 cm. Answer: r = 5; V = 100π ≈ 314.2 cm³.
  5. What happens to cylinder volume if height doubles? Answer: It doubles.
  6. What happens to sphere volume if radius doubles? Answer: It becomes 8 times as large.
  7. A cone and cylinder have the same base and height. Answer: Cone volume is one-third of cylinder volume.
  8. Why is volume written in cubic units? Answer: It measures three-dimensional space (length × width × height).
