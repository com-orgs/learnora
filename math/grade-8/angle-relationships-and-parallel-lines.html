Angle Relationships and Parallel Lines

Angle rules let you calculate unknown angles without measuring them. Learn to identify the relationship before choosing a rule.

Learning time: 20–25 minutes · Level: Developing

Learning goals

  • Use straight-line, full-turn and vertically opposite angle facts to calculate unknown angles.
  • Apply corresponding, alternate and co-interior angle relationships when parallel lines are crossed by a transversal.
  • Use triangle and polygon interior-angle sums to solve problems.
  • Set up and solve simple equations from angle relationships, then check the result.

1. Angles on a straight line

Angles on a straight line add to 180°. If one angle is 127°, the adjacent angle is 53°.

2. Angles around a point

Angles around a point add to 360°. Add the known angles and subtract their total from 360° to find the missing angle.

3. Vertically opposite angles

When two straight lines cross, vertically opposite angles are equal. Adjacent angles on a straight line are supplementary.

4. Parallel lines and a transversal

When a transversal crosses parallel lines, corresponding angles are equal, alternate interior angles are equal, and co-interior angles add to 180°.

5. Triangle and polygon angle sums

The interior angles of a triangle add to 180°. For an n-sided polygon, the interior-angle sum is (n − 2) × 180°. A regular polygon has equal interior angles.

Reasoning with angle relationships

When a transversal crosses two parallel lines, corresponding angles are equal and alternate interior angles are equal. Co-interior angles on the same side of the transversal add to 180°. These relationships let you find missing angles even when the diagram is not drawn to scale.

Worked reasoning: Suppose two corresponding angles are (3x + 10)° and (2x + 35)°. Set them equal: 3x + 10 = 2x + 35, so x = 25. Each angle is 85°. Check by substituting 25 into both expressions.

  • Do not assume two angles are equal just because they look equal in a sketch.
  • Use 180° for a straight line and 360° for a full turn.
  • Write the angle relationship before solving for an unknown.

Self-check: If co-interior angles are 4x° and (2x + 30)°, then 4x + 2x + 30 = 180, so x = 25.

Worked examples

  1. A straight-line pair: 180° − 68° = 112°.
  2. Angles around a point: 90° + 120° + 80° + x = 360°, so x = 70°.
  3. A triangle has angles 45° and 65°; the third is 70°.
  4. A quadrilateral's interior angles add to (4 − 2) × 180° = 360°.

Independent practice

Attempt each question before reviewing the answer.

  1. Find the supplement of 38°. Answer: 142°.
  2. Find the angle around a point if the other angles total 275°. Answer: 85°.
  3. Vertically opposite to 73° is what angle? Answer: 73°.
  4. A triangle has angles 35° and 85°. Find the third. Answer: 60°.
  5. Find the interior-angle sum of a pentagon. Answer: 540°.
  6. Co-interior angle with 112° on parallel lines? Answer: 68°.
  7. A regular hexagon's interior-angle sum is? Answer: 720°; each angle is 120°.
  8. Corresponding angle to 64° on parallel lines? Answer: 64°.

Answer key

  1. 142° because 180° − 38° = 142°.
  2. 85° because 360° − 275° = 85°.
  3. 73°; vertically opposite angles are equal.
  4. 60° because 180° − 35° − 85° = 60°.
  5. 540° because (5 − 2) × 180° = 540°.
  6. 68° because co-interior angles sum to 180°.
  7. 720° total; each regular hexagon angle is 720° ÷ 6 = 120°.
  8. 64° because corresponding angles are equal on parallel lines.

Lesson Summary

  • Angles on a straight line sum to 180° and angles around a point sum to 360°.
  • Vertically opposite angles are equal.
  • For parallel lines, corresponding and alternate angles are equal while co-interior angles sum to 180°.
  • Triangle interior angles total 180°; polygon interior-angle sums depend on the number of sides.
  • Write the angle rule used at each step and check the final value.
