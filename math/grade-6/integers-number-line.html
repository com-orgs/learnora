Integers and the Number Line

Integers include positive whole numbers, negative whole numbers and zero. They help describe temperatures, heights, money changes and positions. Learn to place integers on a number line, compare them and understand opposites and absolute value.

Learning time: approximately 25–30 minutes · Beginner level

What You Will Learn

  • Recognise positive integers, negative integers and zero.
  • Place integers on a number line.
  • Compare and order integers.
  • Understand opposite numbers and absolute value.
  • Connect integers to everyday situations.

1. What Are Integers?

Integers are whole numbers and their negative opposites, including zero. They do not include fractions or decimals.

  • Positive integers: 1, 2, 3, 4, ...
  • Negative integers: −1, −2, −3, −4, ...
  • Zero: 0, which is neither positive nor negative.
Not integers: 1/2, 2.5 and −3.7 are not integers because they are fractions or decimals.

2. Understand the Number Line

On a number line, numbers increase as you move to the right and decrease as you move to the left. Zero is the reference point.

Integers from −5 to 5
−5−4−3−2−1012345

Positive numbers are to the right of zero. Negative numbers are to the left.

3. Compare Integers

The number farther to the right on a number line is greater. This means every positive integer is greater than zero, and zero is greater than every negative integer.

  • 5 > 2 because 5 is to the right of 2.
  • −2 > −5 because −2 is to the right of −5.
  • −3 < 0 because −3 is to the left of zero.
Common mistake: A negative number with a larger absolute value is actually smaller. For example, −8 is less than −3.

4. Order Integers

To order integers from least to greatest, start with the number farthest left on the number line.

Worked example: Order 3, −4, 0, −1 and 2 from least to greatest.

Move from left to right: −4, −1, 0, 2, 3.

From greatest to least, reverse the order: 3, 2, 0, −1, −4.

5. Opposite Integers

Opposite integers are the same distance from zero but on opposite sides of the number line.

  • The opposite of 4 is −4.
  • The opposite of −7 is 7.
  • The opposite of 0 is 0.

Opposite numbers add to zero. For example, 6 + (−6) = 0.

6. Absolute Value

The absolute value of an integer is its distance from zero. Distance is never negative, so absolute value is always zero or positive.

  • |5| = 5
  • |−5| = 5
  • |0| = 0
Important: Absolute value measures distance, not direction. The numbers 5 and −5 have the same absolute value because both are five units from zero.

7. Integers in Everyday Life

Temperature

A temperature of −3°C is three degrees below zero. A temperature of 4°C is above zero. Therefore, 4°C is warmer than −3°C.

Floors in a building

Ground floor can be represented by 0. A basement level may be −1, while a floor above ground may be +2.

Money changes

A deposit of $15 can be represented by +15. Spending $8 can be represented by −8. The total change is +7.

8. Common Mistakes to Avoid

Thinking zero is positive: Zero is neither positive nor negative.
Comparing negative numbers by their digits: On a number line, −2 is greater than −9 because it is farther right.
Making absolute value negative: Absolute value is a distance and cannot be negative.
Mixing opposites and equal numbers: 6 and −6 are opposites, not equal.

9. Practice Exercises

Try these before revealing the answers.

  1. Is −4 positive, negative or zero?
  2. Which is greater: −2 or −6?
  3. Order −3, 4, 0 and −1 from least to greatest.
  4. What is the opposite of −9?
  5. Find |−12|.
  6. Which temperature is warmer: −5°C or −1°C?
  7. A person deposits $20 and then spends $7. What is the total change in money?

12. Lesson Summary

  • Integers are positive whole numbers, negative whole numbers and zero.
  • Numbers increase to the right on a number line.
  • Every positive integer is greater than zero; every negative integer is less than zero.
  • Opposite integers are the same distance from zero on opposite sides.
  • Absolute value is the non-negative distance from zero.
