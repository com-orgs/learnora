GRADE 6 · MATHEMATICS

Multiplying Fractions

Multiplying fractions helps us find a part of another amount. Learn the meaning of fraction multiplication, multiply fractions step by step, simplify your answers and apply the skill to everyday situations.

Learning time: approximately 25–30 minutes · Beginner level

What You Will Learn

  • Understand multiplication as finding a part of a part.
  • Multiply two fractions.
  • Multiply a fraction by a whole number.
  • Simplify products and use cancellation safely.
  • Solve practical word problems.

1. What Does Multiplying Fractions Mean?

Multiplication can describe a fraction of an amount. For example, one half of three quarters means taking half of the three-quarter amount.

Key idea: To multiply fractions, multiply the numerators together and multiply the denominators together. Then simplify the result if possible.
12×34=38

One half of three quarters is three eighths.

2. Multiply Two Fractions

Worked example: 2/3 × 3/5

  1. Multiply the numerators: 2 × 3 = 6.
  2. Multiply the denominators: 3 × 5 = 15.
  3. Write the product: 6/15.
  4. Simplify by dividing top and bottom by 3: 2/5.

Answer: 2/5.

Another example: 3/4 × 2/7

Multiply the numerators: 3 × 2 = 6. Multiply the denominators: 4 × 7 = 28. The product is 6/28, which simplifies to 3/14.

3. Multiply a Fraction by a Whole Number

Write the whole number as a fraction over 1, then multiply.

Worked example: 3 × 2/5

  1. Write 3 as 3/1.
  2. Multiply: 3/1 × 2/5 = 6/5.
  3. As a mixed number, 6/5 = 1 1/5.

Answer: 6/5 or 1 1/5.

Remember: A product can be greater than 1. That is normal when multiplying a fraction by a whole number greater than 1.

4. Simplify Before Multiplying (Cancellation)

You can sometimes make the numbers smaller before multiplying. Divide a numerator and a denominator by the same common factor. You may cancel across the multiplication sign, but only between a numerator and a denominator.

Worked example: 4/9 × 3/8

  1. Look for a common factor between 4 and 8. Divide both by 4: 4 becomes 1 and 8 becomes 2.
  2. Look for a common factor between 3 and 9. Divide both by 3: 3 becomes 1 and 9 becomes 3.
  3. Multiply the simplified fractions: 1/3 × 1/2 = 1/6.

Answer: 1/6.

Important: Cancellation is division by a common factor, not simply crossing out matching digits. Always divide a numerator and a denominator by the same non-zero factor.

5. Solve Everyday Problems

Example 1: A recipe

A recipe uses 3/4 cup of flour. You are making half the recipe. How much flour do you need?

1/2 × 3/4 = 3/8. You need 3/8 cup of flour.

Example 2: A walking route

A walking route is 2/3 kilometre long. Nina walks the route 3 times. How far does she walk?

3 × 2/3 = 6/3 = 2. Nina walks 2 kilometres.

Example 3: A garden

2/5 of a garden is planted with flowers. Red flowers cover 1/2 of the flower area. What fraction of the whole garden has red flowers?

1/2 × 2/5 = 2/10 = 1/5. One fifth of the whole garden has red flowers.

6. Common Mistakes to Avoid

Adding instead of multiplying: For 1/2 × 1/3, multiply to get 1/6. Do not add the numerators or denominators.
Adding denominators: Multiply the denominators; do not add them.
Forgetting to simplify: A product such as 6/15 can be simplified to 2/5.
Incorrect cancellation: Cancel only common factors across a numerator and a denominator, not across two numerators or two denominators.

10. Lesson Summary

  • Multiply the numerators together.
  • Multiply the denominators together.
  • Simplify the product when possible.
  • You may simplify before multiplying by cancelling common factors across numerators and denominators.
  • To find a fraction of an amount, multiply the amount by the fraction.
Next learning goal: Learn how to divide fractions and interpret division in real-life problems.
