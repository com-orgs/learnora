GRADE 6 · MATHEMATICS

Understanding Fractions

Learn what fractions mean, understand their parts, explore visual examples and practise solving fraction questions step by step.

Learning time: approximately 20–25 minutes · Beginner level

Jump to: Explore fractions Number line Practice Quiz Worksheet

What You Will Learn

  • Understand what a fraction represents.
  • Identify the numerator and denominator.
  • Recognise proper, improper and mixed fractions.
  • Read and write fractions using simple examples.
  • Practise identifying fractions in everyday situations.

1. What Is a Fraction?

A fraction represents a part of a whole. It tells us how many equal parts we have out of the total number of equal parts.

Imagine a pizza cut into 4 equal slices. If you eat 1 slice, you have eaten one out of four equal parts.

One out of four equal parts
1 4

The shaded part represents one quarter (1/4).

Fractions are useful in everyday life. We use them when sharing food, measuring ingredients, dividing objects and describing parts of a group.

2. Parts of a Fraction

Look at this fraction:

3 5

Numerator: The top number (3). It tells us how many parts we have.

Denominator: The bottom number (5). It tells us how many equal parts make the whole.

In the fraction 3/5, a whole is divided into 5 equal parts and we are talking about 3 of those parts.

Remember: The denominator tells the total number of equal parts. The numerator tells how many of those parts are being considered.

Explore it yourself: Build a fraction

Change the number of equal parts, then choose how many parts are shaded. Watch how the fraction changes. A fraction only describes a fair share when the parts are equal in size.

Think about it: The denominator stays the same when we shade more pieces. Only the numerator changes.

Fractions on a number line

A fraction is also a number. It tells us a position between whole numbers, not just a shaded piece of a shape.

One whole divided into four equal steps
0
1/4
1/2
3/4
1

The marked point is three equal steps from zero: 3/4.

Worked example: Where is 2/4?
  1. The denominator is 4, so split the distance from 0 to 1 into 4 equal intervals.
  2. The numerator is 2, so count 2 intervals from 0.
  3. You land halfway between 0 and 1. That point is 2/4, which is also 1/2.
Key idea: Equal denominators mean the whole is split into the same number of equal-sized parts. A larger numerator then means more of those parts.

3. Types of Fractions

A. Proper Fractions

A proper fraction has a numerator smaller than its denominator.

Examples: 1/2, 2/5 and 3/4.

Example: In 2/5, 2 is smaller than 5. Therefore, 2/5 is a proper fraction.

B. Improper Fractions

An improper fraction has a numerator greater than or equal to its denominator.

Examples: 5/4, 7/3 and 6/6.

Example: In 7/3, 7 is greater than 3. Therefore, 7/3 is an improper fraction.

C. Mixed Numbers

A mixed number contains a whole number and a proper fraction.

Examples: 1 1/2, 2 1/4 and 3 2/5.

Example: 1 1/2 means one whole and one half.

4. Step-by-Step Solved Examples

Example 1: Identify the numerator and denominator

Question: In the fraction 4/7, identify both parts.

  1. Look at the top number: 4.
  2. Look at the bottom number: 7.
  3. The numerator is 4 and the denominator is 7.

Answer: Numerator = 4; Denominator = 7.

Example 2: Identify the type of fraction

Question: Is 3/8 a proper or improper fraction?

  1. Compare the numerator (3) and denominator (8).
  2. Since 3 is smaller than 8, it is a proper fraction.

Answer: 3/8 is a proper fraction.

Example 3: Write a fraction from a situation

Question: A chocolate bar is divided into 6 equal pieces. Sara eats 2 pieces. What fraction does she eat?

  1. Total equal pieces = 6. This is the denominator.
  2. Pieces eaten = 2. This is the numerator.
  3. Write the fraction as 2/6.

Answer: Sara eats 2/6 of the chocolate bar.

5. Common Mistakes to Avoid

Mistake 1: Mixing up the numerator and denominator.
Remember: numerator is on top; denominator is below.
Mistake 2: Thinking the denominator tells how many parts are selected.
The denominator tells how many equal parts make the whole.
Mistake 3: Dividing something into unequal pieces and calling them equal fractions.
Fractions of a whole require equal-sized parts.
Mistake 4: Assuming every fraction is less than one.
Improper fractions can represent one whole or more than one whole.

9. Lesson Summary

  • A fraction represents part of a whole.
  • The numerator is the top number.
  • The denominator is the bottom number.
  • A proper fraction has a smaller numerator than denominator.
  • An improper fraction has a numerator equal to or greater than its denominator.
  • A mixed number combines a whole number and a proper fraction.
