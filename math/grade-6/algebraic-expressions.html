GRADE 6 · MATHEMATICS

Introduction to Algebraic Expressions

Algebra uses letters to represent numbers. These letters help us describe patterns and write general rules. Learn what variables, constants, coefficients and terms mean, then write and evaluate simple expressions.

Learning time: approximately 30 minutes · Beginner level

What You Will Learn

  • Understand variables, constants, coefficients and terms.
  • Translate simple words into algebraic expressions.
  • Evaluate an expression by substituting a value.
  • Identify and combine like terms.
  • Use algebra to describe simple patterns.

1. What Is Algebra?

Algebra is a part of mathematics that uses letters and symbols to represent numbers and relationships. A letter such as x can stand for a number that is not yet known or for a number that can change.

x + 5

This expression means “a number plus five”. If x is 3, the value is 3 + 5 = 8.

2. Variables, Constants and Coefficients

  • Variable: a letter that represents a number, such as x or n.
  • Constant: a fixed number, such as 7 in x + 7.
  • Coefficient: the number multiplying a variable. In 4x, the coefficient is 4.
Example: In 6a + 9, the variable is a, the coefficient is 6 and the constant is 9.

If a variable appears without a written coefficient, its coefficient is 1. For example, x means 1x.

3. Understand Terms

Terms are parts of an expression separated by addition or subtraction signs.

  • In 3x + 5, the terms are 3x and 5.
  • In 7a − 2b + 4, the terms are 7a, −2b and 4.
Sign matters: In 7a − 2b + 4, the second term is −2b, not just 2b.

Be careful with order: “4 less than a number” means x − 4, not 4 − x.

5. Evaluate an Expression

To evaluate an expression, replace the variable with the given number and calculate using the correct order of operations.

Worked example: Find 3x + 2 when x = 4

  1. Replace x with 4: 3 × 4 + 2.
  2. Multiply first: 12 + 2.
  3. Add: 14.

Another example: Find 2a − 5 when a = 6

2 × 6 − 5 = 12 − 5 = 7.

6. Combine Like Terms

Like terms have the same variable raised to the same power. At this level, focus on terms with the same letter. You can add or subtract their coefficients.

Example: 3x + 2x = 5x because there are 3 x's plus 2 more x's.
  • 4a + 3a = 7a.
  • 8y − 5y = 3y.
  • 2x + 4 + 3x = 5x + 4.
Unlike terms cannot be combined: 3x + 2y cannot be simplified to 5xy or 5x. The variables are different.

7. Describe a Pattern with Algebra

Suppose a pattern starts with 4 tiles and adds 3 tiles each time. If n is the step number, the number of tiles is described by 3n + 1.

8. Common Mistakes to Avoid

Confusing multiplication and addition: 4x means 4 multiplied by x, not 4 + x.
Ignoring the order in a phrase: “5 less than x” is x − 5.
Combining unlike terms: 2x + 3y cannot be combined into 5xy.
Forgetting substitution brackets: When substituting a negative number, use brackets to keep the calculation clear.

12. Lesson Summary

  • Variables are letters that represent numbers.
  • Constants are fixed numbers; coefficients multiply variables.
  • Terms are separated by addition or subtraction signs.
  • Evaluate expressions by substituting values and following operation rules.
  • Combine like terms by adding or subtracting their coefficients.
