GRADES 6–7 · MATHEMATICS

Introduction to Ratios

Learn how a ratio compares two or more quantities. Explore simple pictures, write ratios in different forms, simplify them and solve everyday problems.

Learning time: approximately 25–30 minutes · Beginner level

What You Will Learn

  • Understand what a ratio compares.
  • Write ratios using words, a colon and fractions.
  • Keep the order of quantities correct.
  • Simplify a ratio using a common factor.
  • Use equivalent ratios to solve simple problems.

1. What Is a Ratio?

A ratio is a way to compare two or more quantities. It tells us how much of one quantity there is compared with another.

Imagine a fruit bowl containing 3 apples and 2 oranges. The ratio of apples to oranges is 3 to 2.

3 apples compared with 2 oranges
🍎 🍎 🍎3 apples
🍊 🍊2 oranges

Apples : Oranges = 3 : 2

Ratios are used in recipes, maps, classroom groups, sports and many other everyday situations.

2. How to Write a Ratio

The ratio of 3 apples to 2 oranges can be written in three common ways:

  • Using words: 3 to 2
  • Using a colon: 3 : 2
  • As a fraction: 3/2
Important: The order matters. Apples to oranges is 3 : 2, but oranges to apples is 2 : 3. Always read the quantities in the same order as the question.

3. Ratios with More Than Two Quantities

A ratio can compare three or more quantities. Suppose a class activity uses 2 red counters, 4 blue counters and 3 yellow counters.

2Red
4Blue
3Yellow
Red : Blue : Yellow = 2 : 4 : 3

Keep the quantities in the order given. The ratio has three parts because it compares three groups.

4. Simplifying Ratios

A ratio is in its simplest form when its terms have no common factor greater than 1.

Worked example: Simplify 8 : 12

  1. Find a number that divides both 8 and 12 exactly.
  2. The greatest common factor is 4.
  3. Divide both terms by 4: 8 ÷ 4 = 2 and 12 ÷ 4 = 3.
  4. The simplified ratio is 2 : 3.
Check: 2 : 3 and 8 : 12 are equivalent because multiplying both 2 and 3 by 4 gives 8 and 12.

Another example: Simplify 15 : 25

The greatest common factor of 15 and 25 is 5. Divide both terms by 5 to get 3 : 5.

5. Equivalent Ratios

Equivalent ratios have the same comparison, even though their numbers may be different. Multiply or divide every term by the same non-zero number to make an equivalent ratio.

OperationRatioEquivalent ratio
Start2 : 32 : 3
Multiply both terms by 22 : 34 : 6
Multiply both terms by 32 : 36 : 9
Multiply both terms by 42 : 38 : 12
Avoid this mistake: Do not add the same number to both terms and assume the ratio stays equivalent. For example, 2 : 3 is not equivalent to 3 : 4. Multiply or divide both terms by the same factor instead.

6. Solved Everyday Problems

Example 1: Boys and girls

A club has 10 boys and 15 girls. What is the ratio of boys to girls in simplest form?

  1. Write the ratio in the requested order: 10 : 15.
  2. The greatest common factor is 5.
  3. Divide both terms by 5: 2 : 3.

Answer: 2 : 3.

Example 2: Making a drink

A drink is made using 1 cup of concentrate for every 4 cups of water. How much water is needed for 3 cups of concentrate?

  1. The ratio of concentrate to water is 1 : 4.
  2. Three cups of concentrate is 1 × 3.
  3. Multiply the water amount by the same factor: 4 × 3 = 12.

Answer: 12 cups of water.

Example 3: Find a missing term

Complete the equivalent ratio: 3 : 5 = 12 : ?

  1. 3 has been multiplied by 4 to become 12.
  2. Multiply 5 by the same factor: 5 × 4 = 20.

Answer: 20. The completed ratio is 12 : 20.

7. Common Mistakes to Avoid

Mixing up the order: 4 cats to 7 dogs is 4 : 7, not 7 : 4.
Changing only one term: To make an equivalent ratio, multiply or divide every term by the same factor.
Forgetting to simplify: If both terms have a common factor, divide by it to write the ratio in simplest form.
Confusing a ratio with a total: A ratio of 2 : 3 has 5 total parts when describing a whole split into those two groups, but the ratio itself is still 2 : 3.

8. Practice Exercises

  3. Simplify 6 : 9.
  4. Simplify 14 : 21.
  5. Complete the equivalent ratio: 2 : 5 = 6 : ?
  6. A recipe uses 2 cups of rice for 3 cups of water. How much water is needed for 6 cups of rice?

11. Lesson Summary

  • A ratio compares quantities.
  • The order of terms matters.
  • Ratios can be written using words, a colon or a fraction.
  • Simplify a ratio by dividing every term by the same common factor.
  • Equivalent ratios are made by multiplying or dividing every term by the same non-zero factor.
