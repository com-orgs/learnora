GRADE 6 · MATHEMATICS

Adding and Subtracting Decimals

Adding and subtracting decimals becomes easier when place values are lined up correctly. Learn a reliable written method, use zeros when needed and check your answers with estimation.

Learning time: approximately 25–30 minutes · Beginner level

What You Will Learn

  • Line up decimal points before calculating.
  • Add decimals with different numbers of decimal places.
  • Subtract decimals accurately, including regrouping.
  • Use zeros as placeholders when helpful.
  • Solve money, distance and measurement problems.

1. The Golden Rule: Line Up Decimal Points

When adding or subtracting decimals, digits with the same place value must be in the same column. Ones go under ones, tenths under tenths, and hundredths under hundredths.

Correct alignment
12.45 + 3.70 ------- 16.15

The decimal points are directly below each other.

You can add zeros to the end of a decimal to make the numbers easier to line up. For example, 3.7 = 3.70.

2. Add Decimals

Worked example: 4.35 + 2.8

  1. Write 2.8 as 2.80 so both numbers have two decimal places.
  2. Line up the decimal points.
  3. Add from right to left: hundredths, tenths, ones and then tens if needed.
  4. Keep the decimal point in line in the answer.
4.35 + 2.80 ------ 7.15

Answer: 7.15.

Another example: 0.76 + 0.19

Hundredths: 6 + 9 = 15. Write 5 and carry 1. Tenths: 7 + 1 + 1 = 9. The answer is 0.95.

3. Subtract Decimals

Worked example: 5.2 − 1.76

  1. Write 5.2 as 5.20.
  2. Line up the decimal points.
  3. Subtract from right to left. Regroup from the ones column when needed.
  4. Keep the decimal point in line.
5.20 - 1.76 ------ 3.44

Answer: 3.44.

Check by addition: 3.44 + 1.76 = 5.20. This confirms the subtraction.

4. Understand Regrouping

Sometimes you cannot subtract a digit because the top digit is smaller. Regroup one unit from the place to its left. Remember that one tenth equals ten hundredths, and one whole equals ten tenths.

Example: In 4.30 − 1.85, you cannot subtract 5 hundredths from 0 hundredths. Regroup 1 tenth as 10 hundredths, then continue the subtraction. The answer is 2.45.
Important: Regrouping changes the digits in neighbouring columns, but it does not change the value of the number.

5. Estimate to Check Your Answer

Before or after calculating, round the numbers to easy values. Your exact answer should be reasonably close to the estimate.

Example: 8.72 + 3.19 is approximately 9 + 3 = 12. The exact answer is 11.91, which is close to 12.

Estimation can help you notice a misplaced decimal point or an arithmetic mistake.

6. Solve Everyday Problems

Example 1: Shopping

A notebook costs $2.45 and a pen costs $1.30. What is the total cost?

$2.45 + $1.30 = $3.75.

Example 2: Distance

A runner travels 4.8 km in the morning and 2.65 km in the evening. How far does the runner travel altogether?

4.80 + 2.65 = 7.45 km.

Example 3: Remaining water

A jug contains 3.5 litres of water. A family uses 1.75 litres. How much remains?

3.50 − 1.75 = 1.75 litres.

7. Common Mistakes to Avoid

Watch for these common errors when working with decimal addition and subtraction:

  • Not lining up decimal points: Put each decimal point directly below the others so tenths, hundredths and other place values stay in the correct columns.
  • Leaving out placeholder zeros: Write 3.7 as 3.70 when adding or subtracting hundredths. The value stays the same, but the columns become easier to compare.
  • Moving the decimal point in the answer: Keep the answer's decimal point in line with the points in the numbers above it.
  • Regrouping from the wrong place value: Remember that one whole is ten tenths and one tenth is ten hundredths. Regroup only between neighbouring place-value columns.
  • Skipping an estimate: Round the numbers to easy values first. If the exact answer is far from the estimate, check your alignment and calculation.
Not lining up decimal points: Align place values, not the last digits of the numbers.
Forgetting placeholder zeros: Write 4.6 as 4.60 when adding or subtracting hundredths.
Dropping the decimal point: Bring the decimal point straight down into the answer.
Ignoring regrouping: When a top digit is too small, regroup carefully from the next place to the left.

11. Lesson Summary

  • Line up decimal points before adding or subtracting.
  • Use zeros as placeholders to make place values clear.
  • Add or subtract from right to left, regrouping when needed.
  • Keep the decimal point aligned in the answer.
  • Estimate to check whether your answer is reasonable.
