GRADE 6 · MATHEMATICS

Data and Graphs

Data is information we collect to answer questions. Learn how to organise data in tables, read simple graphs and describe a set of numbers using the mean, median, mode and range.

Learning time: approximately 30 minutes · Beginner level

What You Will Learn

  • Understand data, categories and frequency.
  • Organise information in a frequency table.
  • Read and interpret simple bar charts and line graphs.
  • Calculate mean, median, mode and range.
  • Choose a suitable way to describe a data set.

1. What Is Data?

Data is a collection of facts or measurements. It can be numbers, categories or observations.

  • Numerical data: ages, heights, test scores or temperatures.
  • Categorical data: favourite colours, sports or types of transport.

A survey is one way to collect data. Always think about who was asked and whether the results fairly represent the group you want to understand.

2. Organise Data in a Frequency Table

Frequency means how many times a value or category appears.

Suppose students choose their favourite fruit:

FruitFrequency (students)
Apples6
Bananas4
Oranges5
Grapes3
Total18

The total frequency is 6 + 4 + 5 + 3 = 18 students.

3. Read a Bar Chart

A bar chart compares categories. Each bar represents a category, and its height or length shows the frequency or value. The scale should be clear and the bars should be separated.

Always read the category labels and the scale before interpreting a chart.

4. Read a Line Graph

A line graph is often used to show how something changes over time. Points are plotted and joined in order. Look at the horizontal axis for time and the vertical axis for the measured value.

Example: A line graph of daily temperature can show whether the temperature rose, fell or stayed the same during the week.

Do not assume a line graph proves why a change happened. It shows a pattern, not necessarily the cause.

5. Find the Mean (Average)

The mean is found by adding all the values and dividing by how many values there are.

Mean = total of values ÷ number of values

Worked example: 4, 6, 8 and 10

  1. Add the values: 4 + 6 + 8 + 10 = 28.
  2. There are 4 values.
  3. Divide: 28 ÷ 4 = 7.

Mean = 7.

6. Find the Median

The median is the middle value when the data is arranged in order from smallest to largest.

  • Odd number of values: choose the single middle value.
  • Even number of values: find the mean of the two middle values.
Example 1: 3, 5, 7, 9, 11. The middle value is 7, so the median is 7.
Example 2: 2, 4, 6, 8. The middle values are 4 and 6. Their mean is (4 + 6) ÷ 2 = 5, so the median is 5.

7. Find the Mode and Range

Mode is the value that appears most often. A data set can have one mode, more than one mode or no mode.

Range is the difference between the largest and smallest values.

Example: In 2, 3, 3, 5, 7, the mode is 3. The range is 7 − 2 = 5.

8. Choose the Right Measure

MeasureWhat it tells youHow to find it
MeanAverage valueAdd values, divide by count
MedianMiddle valueOrder data, find the middle
ModeMost frequent valueFind the value appearing most
RangeSpread from lowest to highestLargest minus smallest

The mean can be affected by unusually high or low values. The median can sometimes describe the centre better when the data has an extreme value.

9. Common Mistakes to Avoid

Finding the median before ordering: Always arrange the numbers from smallest to largest first.
Dividing the mean by the wrong number: Count every data value, not the number of different values.
Confusing mode and median: Mode is most frequent; median is the middle value.
Forgetting the range calculation: Subtract the smallest value from the largest value.
Reading a graph without its scale: Check the axis labels and intervals before interpreting it.

10. Practice Exercises

Try these before revealing the answers.

  1. Find the mean of 3, 5, 7 and 9.
  2. Find the median of 2, 8, 4, 6 and 10.
  3. Find the mode of 1, 3, 3, 4, 5, 3 and 6.
  4. Find the range of 12, 5, 8, 15 and 9.
  5. A frequency table shows 4 students chose cats, 7 chose dogs and 3 chose fish. How many students answered?
  6. Find the median of 3, 5, 7 and 11.
  7. Explain one difference between a bar chart and a line graph.
Show practice answers and explanations
  1. (3 + 5 + 7 + 9) ÷ 4 = 24 ÷ 4 = 6.
  2. Order: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10. Median = 6.
  3. Mode = 3 because it appears most often.
  4. 15 − 5 = 10.
  5. 4 + 7 + 3 = 14 students.
  6. The middle values are 5 and 7. Median = (5 + 7) ÷ 2 = 6.
  7. A bar chart compares categories; a line graph often shows change over time.

13. Lesson Summary

  • Data is information collected to answer questions.
  • Frequency tables organise counts for each category or value.
  • Bar charts compare categories; line graphs often show change over time.
  • Mean is the total divided by the number of values.
  • Median is the middle after ordering, mode is most frequent, and range is largest minus smallest.
