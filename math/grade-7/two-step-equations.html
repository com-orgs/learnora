How to Solve Two-Step Equations

Learn how to find an unknown number when an equation requires two inverse operations. You will practise keeping both sides balanced, checking your answer and turning simple word problems into equations.

Learning time: approximately 25–30 minutes · Developing algebra skills

What You Will Learn

  • Recognise the operations in a two-step equation.
  • Use inverse operations in the correct order.
  • Solve equations involving addition, subtraction, multiplication and division.
  • Check a solution by substituting it into the original equation.
  • Write and solve a simple equation from a word problem.

1. What Is an Equation?

An equation is a mathematical statement that says two expressions have the same value. The equals sign (=) means that the left side and right side are balanced.

Example: In x + 4 = 11, the letter x represents an unknown number. The equation asks which number makes both sides equal. The answer is 7 because 7 + 4 = 11.

A two-step equation needs two operations to isolate the unknown. For example, 3x + 5 = 20 includes multiplication by 3 and addition of 5.

2. Understand Inverse Operations

Inverse operations undo one another. Use them to isolate the variable while keeping the equation balanced.

OperationInverse operationExample pair
AdditionSubtraction+ 6 and − 6
SubtractionAddition− 4 and + 4
MultiplicationDivision× 5 and ÷ 5
DivisionMultiplication÷ 3 and × 3
Balance rule: Whatever operation you perform on one side of an equation, perform the same operation on the other side. This keeps the equality true.

3. Solve an Equation with Addition and Multiplication

Worked example: Solve 3x + 5 = 20

Work backwards from the operations around the variable. Undo addition first, then undo multiplication.

Start3x + 5 = 20
Subtract 53x + 5 − 5 = 20 − 5
Simplify3x = 15
Divide by 33x ÷ 3 = 15 ÷ 3
Solutionx = 5
Check: Substitute 5 into the original equation: 3(5) + 5 = 15 + 5 = 20. The left side equals the right side, so x = 5 is correct.

4. Solve an Equation with Subtraction and Multiplication

Worked example: Solve 4x − 7 = 13

  1. Add 7 to both sides to undo subtracting 7: 4x = 20.
  2. Divide both sides by 4: x = 5.
  3. Check: 4(5) − 7 = 20 − 7 = 13.

Answer: x = 5. Notice that addition is used first because it reverses the subtraction outside the term 4x.

5. Solve an Equation with Division

Worked example: Solve x/3 + 4 = 9

  1. Subtract 4 from both sides: x/3 = 5.
  2. Multiply both sides by 3: x = 15.
  3. Check: 15/3 + 4 = 5 + 4 = 9.

Answer: x = 15.

6. When Brackets Are Included

Some equations show a multiplication outside brackets. First simplify the bracket expression or use the distributive property, depending on the method you have learned.

Worked example: Solve 2(x + 3) = 18

  1. Divide both sides by 2: x + 3 = 9.
  2. Subtract 3 from both sides: x = 6.
  3. Check: 2(6 + 3) = 2 × 9 = 18.

Answer: x = 6.

7. Equations with Negative Numbers

Worked example: Solve 5 − 2x = −3

  1. Subtract 5 from both sides: −2x = −8.
  2. Divide both sides by −2: x = 4.
  3. Check: 5 − 2(4) = 5 − 8 = −3.

Answer: x = 4. Remember that a negative divided by a negative gives a positive result.

8. Turn a Word Problem into an Equation

Example: A number is multiplied by 4, then 6 is added. The result is 30. Find the number.

  1. Let the unknown number be x.
  2. “Multiplied by 4” means 4x. “Then 6 is added” means + 6.
  3. Write the equation: 4x + 6 = 30.
  4. Subtract 6: 4x = 24.
  5. Divide by 4: x = 6.
Check the story: 6 × 4 = 24, and 24 + 6 = 30. The number is 6.

9. A Reliable Method

  1. Simplify first if the equation contains brackets or like terms you already know how to combine.
  2. Undo addition or subtraction around the variable.
  3. Undo multiplication or division to isolate the variable.
  4. Check your result in the original equation.

Think of solving as reversing the order of operations. Undo the outside operation first, then continue until the variable is alone.

10. Common Mistakes to Avoid

Doing only one side: If you subtract 5 on the left, subtract 5 on the right too.
Using the wrong inverse: Undo + 7 by subtracting 7, not by adding another 7.
Dividing too early: In 3x + 5 = 20, first remove the + 5. Do not divide 20 by 3 before isolating 3x.
Ignoring negative signs: Keep the sign with its number and check each operation carefully.
Skipping the check: Substitute your answer into the original equation to confirm both sides are equal.

14. Lesson Summary

  • An equation stays balanced when the same operation is applied to both sides.
  • Use inverse operations to isolate the variable.
  • Undo addition or subtraction before multiplication or division in a typical two-step equation.
  • Keep negative signs attached to their numbers.
  • Check the final value in the original equation.
