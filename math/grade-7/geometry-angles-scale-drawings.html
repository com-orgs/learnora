GRADE 7 · GEOMETRY

Geometry: Angles and Scale Drawings

Use angle relationships to calculate missing angles, then apply scale factors to maps, plans and diagrams. You will practise explaining why an angle has a particular size and converting between drawing lengths and real distances.

Estimated learning time: 25–35 minutes · Beginner to intermediate

By the end, you should be able to:

  • Use angle facts on straight lines, around a point and at an intersection.
  • Find angles formed when a transversal crosses parallel lines.
  • Interpret a scale such as 1 : 100 and calculate real or drawing lengths.
  • Use consistent units and scale factors in practical problems.

1. Angle facts you should know

An angle measures a turn and is measured in degrees (°). These facts are useful building blocks for solving geometry problems.

Angle relationshipFact
Angles on a straight lineAdd up to 180°
Angles around a pointAdd up to 360°
Vertically opposite anglesAre equal
Angles in a triangleAdd up to 180°
Angles in a quadrilateralAdd up to 360°
Example A: straight-line angles

Two adjacent angles on a straight line are 127° and x.

x + 127° = 180°

x = 180° − 127° = 53°.

Example B: angles around a point

Three angles around a point are 85°, 140° and x.

85° + 140° + x = 360°

x = 360° − 225° = 135°.

Example C: vertically opposite angles

Two straight lines cross. If one angle is 68°, the angle directly opposite it is also 68°. The two adjacent angles are each 180° − 68° = 112°.

2. Angles made by parallel lines

When a straight line called a transversal crosses two parallel lines, special angle relationships appear.

  • Corresponding angles are equal. They occupy matching positions at the two intersections.
  • Alternate interior angles are equal. They lie between the parallel lines on opposite sides of the transversal.
  • Co-interior angles add up to 180°. They lie between the parallel lines on the same side of the transversal.
Example D: use alternate angles

Two parallel lines are crossed by a transversal. One interior angle is 73°. Its alternate interior angle is also 73°.

Example E: use co-interior angles

If one co-interior angle is 118°, the other is 180° − 118° = 62°.

How to solve: mark the parallel lines, identify the angle pair, write the angle fact, and then calculate. Do not assume lines are parallel unless the diagram or question tells you.

3. What is a scale drawing?

A scale drawing represents a real object or distance using a smaller or larger drawing. The scale compares a length on the drawing with the corresponding real length, using the same units.

A scale of 1 : 100 means 1 unit on the drawing represents 100 of the same units in real life. For example, 1 cm on a floor plan represents 100 cm (1 m) in the room.

ScaleMeaningExample
1 : 501 drawing unit = 50 real units2 cm represents 100 cm
1 : 1001 drawing unit = 100 real units3 cm represents 300 cm
1 : 25,0001 map unit = 25,000 real units1 cm represents 25,000 cm = 250 m
Example F: drawing length to real length

A plan uses a scale of 1 : 100. A wall measures 4.5 cm on the plan.

Real length = 4.5 × 100 = 450 cm = 4.5 m.

Example G: real length to drawing length

A table is 180 cm long. On a drawing with scale 1 : 30, its drawing length is 180 ÷ 30 = 6 cm.

Unit check: convert units before calculating. If a map scale gives centimetres but the answer is requested in kilometres, convert the final distance carefully. 100 cm = 1 m and 1,000 m = 1 km.

4. Scale factors and enlargements

A scale factor tells you how many times a length changes. Multiply an original length by the scale factor to find the new length. A factor greater than 1 enlarges; a positive factor between 0 and 1 reduces.

Example H: enlargement

A rectangle is 3 cm wide and 5 cm long. It is enlarged by scale factor 2.

New width = 3 × 2 = 6 cm. New length = 5 × 2 = 10 cm.

Every length is doubled, so the shape keeps the same proportions.

Example I: reduction

A 12 cm line is reduced by scale factor 0.5. New length = 12 × 0.5 = 6 cm.

For a scale drawing written as 1 : n, divide the real length by n to get the drawing length, or multiply the drawing length by n to get the real length. Always check which direction the question asks you to convert.

5. Plan a room using a scale

Suppose a rectangular room is 5 m long and 3 m wide. You want to draw it using a scale of 1 : 100.

  1. Convert real measurements to centimetres: 5 m = 500 cm and 3 m = 300 cm.
  2. Divide each measurement by 100.
  3. Draw a rectangle 5 cm long and 3 cm wide.

The drawing is proportional to the real room. If you add a door or window, measure its real position and convert that distance using the same scale.

6. Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using 360° for a straight line. Angles on a straight line total 180°; angles around a point total 360°.
  • Assuming adjacent angles are equal. Only use an equality rule when the angle relationship supports it.
  • Mixing up alternate and co-interior angles. Alternate angles are equal; co-interior angles total 180° when the lines are parallel.
  • Multiplying when you should divide in a scale problem. Decide whether you are finding the real length or the drawing length.
  • Forgetting to convert units. A scale ratio compares like units.
  • Changing only one side of a shape during an enlargement. Apply the same scale factor to every length.

7. Practice questions

Try each question before opening the solution.

  1. Two angles lie on a straight line. One is 42°. Find the other.
    Show solution

    180° − 42° = 138°.

  2. Three angles around a point are 90°, 125° and x. Find x.
    Show solution

    x = 360° − 90° − 125° = 145°.

  3. Two lines cross. One angle is 114°. Find the vertically opposite angle and one adjacent angle.
    Show solution

    Vertically opposite angle = 114°. Adjacent angle = 180° − 114° = 66°.

  4. Parallel lines are crossed by a transversal. An alternate interior angle is 57°. Find its partner.
    Show solution

    Alternate interior angles are equal, so the answer is 57°.

  5. A map scale is 1 : 20,000. Two places are 3 cm apart on the map. Find the real distance in metres.
    Show solution

    3 × 20,000 = 60,000 cm. Divide by 100: 600 m.

  6. A 7 m wall is shown on a plan at scale 1 : 100. How long is it on the plan in centimetres?
    Show solution

    7 m = 700 cm. 700 ÷ 100 = 7 cm.

  7. A drawing of a triangle has sides 4 cm, 5 cm and 6 cm. It is enlarged by scale factor 3. Find the new side lengths.
    Show solution

    4 × 3 = 12 cm, 5 × 3 = 15 cm, and 6 × 3 = 18 cm. New sides: 12 cm, 15 cm and 18 cm.

  8. A rectangular garden is 8 m by 6 m. Draw it at scale 1 : 200. Give both drawing dimensions in centimetres.
    Show solution

    8 m = 800 cm; 800 ÷ 200 = 4 cm. 6 m = 600 cm; 600 ÷ 200 = 3 cm. Drawing: 4 cm by 3 cm.
