Area, Surface Area and Volume

Area measures a flat surface, surface area measures the outside of a three-dimensional object, and volume measures the space inside it. Learn how to choose the right formula, show your working and use correct square or cubic units.

Estimated learning time: 30–40 minutes · Beginner to intermediate

Learning goals

  • Calculate the area of common two-dimensional shapes.
  • Find the surface area of cubes, cuboids and simple prisms.
  • Calculate the volume of cubes, cuboids and prisms.
  • Convert units and solve practical measurement problems.

1. Area, surface area and volume: what is the difference?

MeasurementWhat it measuresTypical units
AreaThe amount of flat surface inside a 2D shapecm², m², km²
Surface areaThe total area of all outside faces of a 3D objectcm², m²
VolumeThe space occupied or capacity inside a 3D objectcm³, m³

Imagine covering a floor with tiles: you need area. Painting every outside face of a box requires surface area. Filling the box with sand requires volume.

Units matter: area uses square units because it has two dimensions. Volume uses cubic units because it has three dimensions. Do not write cm for an area or cm² for a volume.

2. Area of common 2D shapes

Use the perpendicular height, not a sloping side, when a formula requires height.

ShapeArea formulaWhat the letters mean
Rectanglelength × widthl is length; w is width
Squareside × side = s²s is one side
Triangle½ × base × perpendicular heightb is base; h is perpendicular height
Parallelogrambase × perpendicular heighth meets the base at 90°
Trapezium (trapezoid)½ × (a + b) × ha and b are parallel sides
Circleπ × r²r is radius; π ≈ 3.14
Example A: rectangle

A rectangular garden is 8 m long and 5 m wide.

Area = 8 × 5 = 40 m²

The garden covers 40 square metres.

Example B: triangle

A triangle has base 10 cm and perpendicular height 6 cm.

Area = ½ × 10 × 6 = 30 cm²

Example C: parallelogram

The base is 9 cm and perpendicular height is 4 cm.

Area = 9 × 4 = 36 cm²

Do not multiply the base by the sloping side.

Example D: trapezium

The parallel sides are 7 cm and 11 cm. The perpendicular height is 5 cm.

Area = ½ × (7 + 11) × 5 = 45 cm²

Example E: circle

A circle has radius 3 cm.

Area = π × 3² = 9π ≈ 28.27 cm²

Radius is half the diameter. If the diameter is given, divide it by 2 first.

The triangle's height meets its base at a right angle.

3. Composite shapes

A composite shape is made from two or more familiar shapes. Split it into simpler parts, calculate each area, and then add or subtract as the diagram requires.

Example F: an L-shaped floor

Imagine a 10 m by 8 m rectangle with a 4 m by 3 m rectangle removed from one corner.

Large rectangle area = 10 × 8 = 80 m².

Removed rectangle area = 4 × 3 = 12 m².

L-shape area = 80 − 12 = 68 m²

Strategy: mark every missing length before calculating. If a composite shape is split into non-overlapping pieces, add their areas. If a cut-out is removed, subtract its area.

4. Surface area of 3D objects

Surface area is the sum of the areas of every outside face. A net is a flat layout of those faces that can fold into a 3D object.

Example G: cube

A cube has six equal square faces. If each edge is 4 cm, one face has area 4 × 4 = 16 cm².

Surface area = 6 × 16 = 96 cm²

Formula: 6s².

Example H: cuboid

A box is 5 cm long, 3 cm wide and 2 cm high. Its faces come in three matching pairs.

Top and bottom: 2 × (5 × 3) = 30 cm².

Front and back: 2 × (5 × 2) = 20 cm².

Left and right: 2 × (3 × 2) = 12 cm².

Total surface area = 30 + 20 + 12 = 62 cm²

Formula: 2(lw + lh + wh).

Example I: triangular prism

A prism has a triangular cross-section with area 12 cm² and perimeter 12 cm. The prism is 8 cm long.

Two triangular ends: 2 × 12 = 24 cm².

Three rectangular side faces together: perimeter × length = 12 × 8 = 96 cm².

Surface area = 24 + 96 = 120 cm²

Net method: calculate the area of each face once and add them. Check that no face is missing or counted twice. For a prism, the two ends are identical, and the side area is the perimeter of the cross-section multiplied by the prism length.

5. Volume of cubes, cuboids and prisms

Volume tells you how many cubic units fit inside a solid. For a prism, the cross-section stays the same along its length.

SolidVolume formula
Cubeside × side × side = s³
Cuboidlength × width × height
Any prismarea of cross-section × length
Cylinderπ × radius² × height
Example J: cuboid

A storage box is 8 cm long, 3 cm wide and 4 cm high.

Volume = 8 × 3 × 4 = 96 cm³

Example K: triangular prism

The triangular cross-section has base 6 cm and perpendicular height 4 cm. The prism is 10 cm long.

Triangle area = ½ × 6 × 4 = 12 cm².

Volume = 12 × 10 = 120 cm³

Example L: cylinder

A cylinder has radius 2 cm and height 7 cm.

Volume = π × 2² × 7 = 28π ≈ 87.96 cm³

Prism shortcut: volume = cross-sectional area × length. This works for a cuboid, triangular prism and other prisms when the cross-section is constant along the length.

6. Units and conversions

Convert measurements before substituting into a formula. For area and volume, the conversion factor must also be squared or cubed.

LengthAreaVolume
1 m = 100 cm1 m² = 10,000 cm²1 m³ = 1,000,000 cm³
1 cm = 10 mm1 cm² = 100 mm²1 cm³ = 1,000 mm³
Example M: convert square units

Convert 2 m² into cm². Since 1 m = 100 cm, then 1 m² = 100 × 100 = 10,000 cm².

2 m² = 2 × 10,000 = 20,000 cm².

Example N: convert cubic units

Convert 0.5 m³ into cm³. Since 1 m = 100 cm, then 1 m³ = 100 × 100 × 100 = 1,000,000 cm³.

0.5 m³ = 500,000 cm³.

Do not convert area like length: 1 m² is not 100 cm². It is 10,000 cm² because both dimensions are converted.

7. Choose the right measurement

  • How much carpet covers a room? Find area.
  • How much paint covers the outside of a box? Find surface area, then consider paint coverage if required.
  • How much water fits inside a tank? Find volume or capacity.

For water, 1 cm³ = 1 mL and 1,000 cm³ = 1 L. Also, 1 m³ = 1,000 L. These links are useful when a volume question asks for liquid capacity.

Example O: tank capacity

A rectangular tank measures 50 cm × 30 cm × 40 cm.

Volume = 50 × 30 × 40 = 60,000 cm³.

Since 1,000 cm³ = 1 L, capacity = 60 L.

8. Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using a sloping side instead of perpendicular height in a triangle or parallelogram.
  • Forgetting the ½ in the triangle and trapezium formulas.
  • Finding only one face of a solid when the question asks for total surface area.
  • Using a square unit for volume or a cubic unit for area.
  • Mixing centimetres and metres in the same calculation without converting.
  • Multiplying an area conversion by 100 instead of 100².
  • Confusing radius with diameter in a circle formula.

9. Practice questions

Attempt each problem before revealing the worked answer.

  1. A rectangle is 12 cm long and 7 cm wide. Find its area.
    Show solution

    12 × 7 = 84 cm².

  2. A triangle has base 9 m and perpendicular height 4 m. Find its area.
    Show solution

    ½ × 9 × 4 = 18 m².

  3. A trapezium has parallel sides 6 cm and 10 cm, with height 5 cm. Find its area.
    Show solution

    ½ × (6 + 10) × 5 = 40 cm².

  4. A cube has edges of 3 cm. Find its total surface area.
    Show solution

    6 × 3² = 6 × 9 = 54 cm².

  5. A cuboid measures 6 cm × 4 cm × 2 cm. Find its total surface area.
    Show solution

    2(6×4 + 6×2 + 4×2) = 2(24 + 12 + 8) = 88 cm².

  6. A box measures 9 cm × 5 cm × 4 cm. Find its volume.
    Show solution

    9 × 5 × 4 = 180 cm³.

  7. A triangular prism has cross-sectional area 15 cm² and length 8 cm. Find its volume.
    Show solution

    15 × 8 = 120 cm³.

  8. Convert 3 m² into cm².
    Show solution

    1 m² = 10,000 cm², so 3 m² = 30,000 cm².
