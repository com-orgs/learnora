Transformations and Symmetry

A transformation changes the position or orientation of a shape. Explore translations, reflections and rotations, then identify lines of symmetry and rotational symmetry while keeping track of lengths and angles.

Learning time: approximately 30–40 minutes · Curriculum-neutral learning support

What You Will Learn

  • Describe translations, reflections and rotations accurately.
  • Use direction, distance, centre and angle to specify a transformation.
  • Recognise line symmetry and rotational symmetry.
  • Understand that a rigid transformation preserves lengths and angles.

1. What Is a Transformation?

A transformation maps every point of a shape to a new position. The original shape is the object; its image is the shape after the transformation. In a rigid transformation, corresponding side lengths and angles stay equal, so the image is congruent to the object.

Key idea: A transformation can move or turn a shape without stretching or changing its size.

2. Translation: Slide a Shape

A translation slides every point the same distance in the same direction. It does not turn or flip the shape.

On a coordinate grid, a translation by vector (a, b) maps (x, y) to (x + a, y + b).

Example: Translate point A(2, 3) by vector (4, −1). New coordinates are (2 + 4, 3 − 1) = (6, 2).

Positive horizontal movement is to the right; negative is to the left. Positive vertical movement is up; negative is down.

3. Reflection: Flip Across a Line

A reflection flips a shape across a mirror line. Each point and its image lie on opposite sides of the mirror line at equal perpendicular distances.

Reflection lineCoordinate rule
x-axis(x, y) → (x, −y)
y-axis(x, y) → (−x, y)
line y = x(x, y) → (y, x)
Example: Reflect (−3, 5) in the y-axis. Change the sign of x: image = (3, 5).

4. Rotation: Turn Around a Centre

A rotation turns a shape around a fixed centre through a specified angle and direction. A complete turn is 360°. Common turns are 90°, 180° and 270°.

For rotations about the origin (0, 0), anticlockwise rules include:

RotationCoordinate rule
90° anticlockwise(x, y) → (−y, x)
180°(x, y) → (−x, −y)
270° anticlockwise(x, y) → (y, −x)
Example: Rotate (2, 1) by 90° anticlockwise about the origin: (2, 1) → (−1, 2).

Always state the centre, angle and direction. A clockwise 90° turn is equivalent to an anticlockwise 270° turn.

5. Line Symmetry

A shape has line symmetry if it can be folded along a line so that the two halves match exactly. The fold line is called a line of symmetry.

  • A square has 4 lines of symmetry.
  • A non-square rectangle has 2 lines of symmetry.
  • An equilateral triangle has 3 lines of symmetry.
  • A general scalene triangle has no line of symmetry.

Some shapes have no line symmetry; others have several. Check whether each half is a mirror image, not merely similar in appearance.

6. Rotational Symmetry

A shape has rotational symmetry if it matches its original position after a turn of less than 360°. The order is the number of times it matches during one complete turn.

Example: A square matches at 90°, 180°, 270° and 360°. Its rotational symmetry has order 4. Its smallest positive angle of rotation is 90°.

For a shape with rotational symmetry, smallest angle = 360° ÷ order.

7. Describe a Transformation Clearly

Use precise mathematical language so another person can reproduce the transformation.

  • Translation: give the vector or direction and distance.
  • Reflection: name the mirror line.
  • Rotation: give the centre, angle and direction.
Common mistake: Saying only “turn the shape” is incomplete. State how far it turns, which way, and around which centre.

8. Practice Exercises

Try these questions before revealing the worked solutions.

  1. Translate (−1, 4) by vector (3, −2).
  2. Reflect (5, −2) in the x-axis.
  3. Reflect (−4, 1) in the y-axis.
  4. Rotate (3, 2) by 90° anticlockwise about the origin.
  5. Rotate (−2, 5) by 180° about the origin.
  6. How many lines of symmetry does a non-square rectangle have?
  7. A shape has rotational symmetry of order 6. Find its smallest positive angle of rotation.
  8. Explain why a translation is a rigid transformation.
Show practice answers and explanations
  1. (−1 + 3, 4 − 2) = (2, 2).
  2. Change the sign of y: (5, 2).
  3. Change the sign of x: (4, 1).
  4. (x, y) → (−y, x), so (3, 2) → (−2, 3).
  5. (x, y) → (−x, −y), so (−2, 5) → (2, −5).
  6. 2 lines of symmetry.
  7. 360° ÷ 6 = 60°.
  8. Every point moves by the same vector, so distances and angles between points are unchanged.
