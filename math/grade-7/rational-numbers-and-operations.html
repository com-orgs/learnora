Rational Numbers and Operations

Rational numbers include integers, fractions and terminating or repeating decimals. They can be positive, negative or zero. Learn how to recognise, compare and calculate with rational numbers using reliable methods and clear worked examples.

Learning time: approximately 35–40 minutes · Developing level · Includes practice, quiz and printable worksheet

What You Will Learn

  • Recognise rational numbers in fraction, decimal and integer form.
  • Understand positive and negative rational values.
  • Compare and order rational numbers.
  • Add and subtract rational numbers.
  • Multiply and divide rational numbers.
  • Use operation rules to solve everyday problems and check answers.

1. What Is a Rational Number?

A rational number is any number that can be written as a fraction a/b, where a and b are integers and b is not zero.

3/4−2/570.60

All of these are rational numbers because each can be written as a fraction of integers.

  • 3/4 is already a fraction.
  • −2 can be written as −2/1.
  • 0.6 = 6/10 = 3/5.
  • 0 = 0/1.
  • 0.333... = 1/3, so a repeating decimal is rational.

A decimal that terminates or repeats can be written as a fraction. A non-terminating, non-repeating decimal is not rational.

2. Positive, Negative and Zero

Rational numbers can appear on either side of zero on a number line.

  • Positive: greater than zero, such as 1/2 or 2.4.
  • Negative: less than zero, such as −3/4 or −1.2.
  • Zero: neither positive nor negative.
Real-life example: A bank transaction of +$12 represents money added. A transaction of −$5 represents money taken away. The signs show direction or change.

Opposites have the same distance from zero but different signs. For example, 3/5 and −3/5 are opposites.

3. Compare and Order Rational Numbers

To compare rational numbers, it can help to write them as fractions with a common denominator or convert them to decimals. On a number line, the number farther right is greater.

Example A: Compare 2/3 and 3/4.

Use a common denominator of 12:

  • 2/3 = 8/12
  • 3/4 = 9/12

Since 8/12 is less than 9/12, 2/3 < 3/4.

Example B: Compare −1/2 and −3/4.

Convert to decimals: −1/2 = −0.5 and −3/4 = −0.75. Since −0.5 is farther right on the number line, −1/2 > −3/4.

Watch negative values: The negative number with the larger absolute value is smaller. For example, −0.8 is less than −0.3.

4. Add Rational Numbers

When adding fractions, use a common denominator. With signed numbers, remember that adding a negative is like moving left on the number line.

Same sign

Example: −2/7 + (−3/7) = −5/7. Add the absolute values and keep the negative sign.

Different signs

Example: 5/8 + (−1/8) = 4/8 = 1/2. Subtract the smaller absolute value from the larger and keep the sign of the number with the larger absolute value.

Unlike denominators

Example: 1/3 + 1/4.
  1. Use 12 as the common denominator.
  2. 1/3 = 4/12 and 1/4 = 3/12.
  3. 4/12 + 3/12 = 7/12.

5. Subtract Rational Numbers

Subtracting a number is the same as adding its opposite. Keep the first number, change subtraction to addition, and change the sign of the second number.

Example A: 3/5 − 1/5 = 2/5.
Example B: 2/3 − (−1/3) = 2/3 + 1/3 = 1.
Example C: −1/4 − 1/2 = −1/4 − 2/4 = −3/4.
Common mistake: Do not subtract denominators. Find a common denominator, then add or subtract the numerators.

6. Multiply Rational Numbers

To multiply fractions, multiply the numerators together and the denominators together. You can simplify before or after multiplying.

Example A: 2/3 × 3/5 = 6/15 = 2/5.

For signed numbers:

  • Positive × positive = positive.
  • Negative × negative = positive.
  • Positive × negative = negative.
  • Negative × positive = negative.
Example B: (−2/3) × (3/4) = −6/12 = −1/2.
Example C: (−1/2) × (−4/5) = 4/10 = 2/5.

7. Divide Rational Numbers

To divide by a fraction, multiply by its reciprocal. The reciprocal swaps the numerator and denominator. A number cannot be divided by zero.

Example A: 2/3 ÷ 4/5.
  1. Keep the first fraction: 2/3.
  2. Change division to multiplication and flip the second fraction: 5/4.
  3. Multiply: 2/3 × 5/4 = 10/12 = 5/6.
Example B: (−3/4) ÷ (1/2) = (−3/4) × (2/1) = −6/4 = −3/2.
Remember: Only flip the divisor (the second fraction), not the first fraction. Division by zero is undefined.

8. Use the Order of Operations

When an expression has more than one operation, use the agreed order: parentheses first, then exponents, then multiplication and division from left to right, then addition and subtraction from left to right.

Example: 1/2 + 2 × 1/4.
  1. Multiply first: 2 × 1/4 = 2/4 = 1/2.
  2. Add: 1/2 + 1/2 = 1.

Do not automatically work from left to right when operations have different priorities.

9. Rational Numbers in Everyday Situations

Temperature change

The temperature is −2.5°C and rises by 4°C. Calculate −2.5 + 4 = 1.5°C.

Sharing a quantity

You have 3/4 of a pizza and share it equally among 3 people. Calculate 3/4 ÷ 3 = 3/4 × 1/3 = 1/4 of a pizza each.

Distance below sea level

A diver is at −2.4 metres and descends another 1.3 metres. The new position is −2.4 + (−1.3) = −3.7 metres.

10. Common Mistakes to Avoid

Adding denominators: 1/4 + 1/4 = 2/4, not 2/8. Keep the common denominator.
Forgetting signs: Write negative signs clearly and apply the sign rules consistently.
Flipping the wrong fraction: When dividing, take the reciprocal of the second fraction only.
Dividing by zero: Division by zero is undefined.
Rounding too early: Keep exact fractions during calculations where possible, then convert or round only if the question asks.

14. Lesson Summary

  • A rational number can be written as a fraction of integers with a non-zero denominator.
  • Rational numbers may be positive, negative or zero.
  • Use common denominators to add or subtract fractions.
  • Multiply numerators and denominators to multiply fractions.
  • Divide by a fraction by multiplying by its reciprocal.
  • Apply sign rules and the order of operations carefully.
