How to Check AI Answers and Sources

AI tools can explain ideas, but they can also invent details, mix up sources or omit context. Treat a generated response as a starting point and verify important claims using reliable evidence.

Learning time: 15–20 minutes · Beginner level · Free study resource

What you will learn

  • Separate factual claims from opinions.
  • Trace claims to original trustworthy sources.
  • Check dates and whether evidence supports a claim.
  • Explain uncertainty and cite sources honestly.

1. Break an answer into claims

Do not verify a long response all at once. Identify each important factual statement. Check names, dates, numbers, quotations and causal claims separately.

If an answer says a law began in a certain year and applies in every country, those are two different claims. Geographic scope needs its own evidence.

2. Inspect the source

Prefer primary sources when possible: official government pages for laws, original research for a study and recognised scientific organisations for scientific explanations.

Open the source rather than relying only on a search snippet. Check who published it, when it was updated and whether it actually supports the claim.

3. Record uncertainty

If sources disagree, explain the difference. If a source cannot be found, say the claim remains unverified. Never invent a citation or imply you read a source you did not inspect.

For school work, follow citation rules and disclose AI assistance when required. Your final explanation should show your own understanding.

Common mistakes to avoid

Trusting a generated citation without opening it.
Using an outdated source for changing information.
Citing a page that does not support the exact claim.

Practice exercises

1. Turn one factual statement into a question to verify.

2. Name two signs a source may be unreliable.

3. Explain what to do when a claim cannot be confirmed.

Knowledge check: five questions

Choose one answer for each question. Feedback explains every answer.

1. What should you do with an important AI claim?
2. Best source for the wording of a law?
3. Why check a source date?
4. What if a generated citation cannot be found?
5. What must a source do to support a claim?
