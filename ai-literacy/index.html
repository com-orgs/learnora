AI LITERACY · GRADES 6–8

Artificial Intelligence: A Student's Guide

Artificial intelligence (AI) describes computer systems designed to perform tasks that normally require aspects of human intelligence, such as recognising patterns, processing language or making predictions. AI tools can be useful, but their outputs are not automatically true, fair or complete.

This guide teaches students to understand AI, question its answers and protect their privacy. It does not encourage students to bypass school rules or submit AI-generated work as their own.

Six important AI literacy ideas

1. AI learns patterns from data

Many AI systems use examples or data to detect patterns and produce predictions or generated outputs. The training data and system design affect results.

2. AI can make mistakes

A fluent answer can still contain errors, missing context or invented details. Check important claims against trusted sources.

3. Prompts give instructions

A prompt is the question or instruction supplied to an AI tool. Clear context and specific requirements can make an answer more useful, but cannot guarantee accuracy.

4. Bias and fairness matter

Data and design choices can produce unfair or incomplete results. Ask who may be missing from the data and who could be affected.

5. Protect personal information

Do not enter passwords, private contact details, school records, financial information or another person's personal data into public AI tools.

6. People remain responsible

Humans choose when to use AI, review its output, consider consequences and follow school, family and platform rules.

Example: check an AI answer before using it

  1. Identify the exact claim the AI has made.
  2. Find a reliable source such as a textbook, official education resource or recognised scientific organisation.
  3. Check whether the source supports the claim and whether the date or context matters.
  4. Correct the answer, explain uncertainty and cite the source when required.
Remember: AI is a tool for learning and exploration—not a replacement for evidence, independent thinking or your teacher's instructions.

Questions students often ask about AI

Can AI always give correct answers?

No. AI systems can misunderstand a question, use outdated information or generate plausible-sounding errors. Verify important information independently.

Is it okay to use AI for homework?

That depends on the teacher's and school's rules. If AI assistance is allowed, be transparent about how it was used and make sure the final work reflects your own understanding.

Can I share my personal details with an AI chatbot?

Avoid sharing private or identifying information. Use age-appropriate tools with a parent or teacher's guidance and review the service's privacy settings and rules.
