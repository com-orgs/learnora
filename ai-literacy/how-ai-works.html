How Artificial Intelligence Works

Artificial intelligence is a broad field in which computer systems perform tasks such as recognising patterns, processing language or making predictions. Many modern systems learn statistical patterns from examples and use them to produce outputs.

Learning time: 15–20 minutes · Beginner level · Free study resource

What you will learn

  • Describe AI as computer techniques, not a human mind.
  • Explain the role of data in machine learning.
  • Distinguish inputs, models and outputs.
  • Explain why AI results need human review.

1. Data and patterns

A machine-learning system is trained using examples. A model adjusts internal parameters to capture patterns in training data. A model trained on labelled images may learn to classify new images.

The quality and coverage of data affect what a model learns. Missing cases can lead to weak or unfair results.

2. Training and using a model

Training adjusts a model using data and an objective. Later, the model receives new input and produces an output such as a classification, prediction or generated text.

An output is not a guaranteed fact. It depends on the model, input, training and design. Results can be incorrect, incomplete or outdated.

3. People and responsibility

People choose tasks, prepare data, design systems, evaluate results and decide how outputs are used. Human oversight matters when results affect safety, education or access to services.

Use AI as a learning aid, not an authority. Ask what evidence supports an answer, what may be missing and whether a person should make the final decision.

Common mistakes to avoid

Assuming every AI system works the same way.
Thinking fluent output must be correct.
Ignoring missing or unbalanced training data.

Practice exercises

1. Give an input and output for an image classifier.

2. Explain why training examples matter.

3. Name one reason to review an AI answer.

Knowledge check: five questions

Choose one answer for each question. Feedback explains every answer.

1. What do many machine-learning systems learn from?
2. What is a model?
3. Does AI output guarantee truth?
4. Why does training data matter?
5. Who is responsible for decisions using AI?
