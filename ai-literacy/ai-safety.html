AI Bias, Privacy and Safe Use

AI tools can affect what people see, learn and decide. Responsible use means understanding limitations, noticing unfairness and protecting personal information. Follow age requirements, school rules and guidance from a trusted adult.

Learning time: 15–20 minutes · Beginner level · Free study resource

What you will learn

  • Explain how incomplete data can produce unfair results.
  • Recognise sensitive personal information.
  • Identify safer ways to use AI for learning.
  • Know when to ask an adult or teacher for help.

1. Understand bias and fairness

A system may produce uneven results when its training data does not represent the people or situations where it is used. Design choices and evaluation also matter.

Ask whose experiences are included, who may be missing and who could be harmed by an error. Testing should cover different relevant groups and situations.

2. Protect privacy

Do not enter passwords, one-time codes, home addresses, private contact details, school records or financial information into a public AI tool.

Check age rules, privacy information and school guidance. Use only the minimum information needed, and ask a parent, guardian or teacher if unsure.

3. Use AI honestly

AI may help explain a concept or create practice questions when its use is allowed. It should not replace your thinking or be submitted as your work when that breaks school rules.

Check answers, disclose assistance when required, and tell a trusted adult about concerning interactions or unsafe requests.

Common mistakes to avoid

Assuming automated systems are always fair.
Sharing private information to get a tailored answer.
Submitting generated work as your own against school rules.

Practice exercises

1. Name information you should not share in a public chatbot.

2. Explain how missing examples can affect fairness.

3. Give one responsible use of AI for revision.

Knowledge check: five questions

Choose one answer for each question. Feedback explains every answer.

1. What can cause unfair AI results?
2. What should you avoid sharing with a public AI tool?
3. What is a useful fairness question?
4. How should students use AI for homework?
5. What if an AI tool asks for sensitive information?
