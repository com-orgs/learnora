Editorial and Corrections Policy

Learnora is an independent supplementary learning resource. This page explains how educational material is prepared, what our checks can and cannot establish, how sources are used and how readers can report possible errors.

Policy last updated: 3 November 2026.

How content is prepared

Lessons are written to explain concepts in accessible language and may use AI-assisted drafting. Editors aim to include learning objectives, explanations, worked examples, independent practice, quizzes and suggested worksheet answers where appropriate. Automated tools may check page structure, links, quiz answer mappings, JavaScript behaviour and responsive layout.

Automated checks can identify technical inconsistencies, but they do not prove that every explanation, calculation, question or answer is academically correct. A passing software test is not a substitute for review by a qualified subject educator.

Sources and curriculum scope

Where a lesson discusses a curriculum framework or relies on factual scientific guidance, we aim to link to relevant primary sources such as government curriculum documents, standards bodies and public scientific agencies.

A link to a curriculum document does not by itself mean a Learnora lesson is formally aligned to every standard, grade expectation or local teaching sequence. Unless a page explicitly documents a verified mapping, treat the lesson as supplementary practice and follow the learner's school guidance.

Readers should prefer the latest official document published by their own education authority where local requirements differ.

Reviewers and transparency

We will publish a named reviewer only after that person has actually reviewed and approved the relevant material, and has agreed to be identified. A reviewer record should state the person's name, relevant role or qualifications, the subject and scope reviewed, and the review date.

Current status: Learnora does not currently claim that its lessons have been independently reviewed by named subject experts. Technical QA, editorial desk checks and source-link checks must not be mistaken for independent academic certification.

Corrections process

  1. Email Learnora.site@gmail.com.
  2. Include the page URL, heading or question number, and exact statement you believe is incorrect.
  3. Explain the suggested correction and, where possible, include a reliable primary source or textbook reference.
  4. We assess the report, check the relevant material and update the page when a correction is warranted.
  5. For material changes, we aim to record the date, affected page, nature of the correction and supporting source in our internal editorial log.

Please do not send passwords, private student records or unnecessary personal information.

Quizzes and worksheets

Quiz answer keys and worksheet suggestions support self-study, not teacher marking or formal assessment. We check answer-key mappings and interactive features, but this does not establish independent subject-expert validation. Report ambiguous or incorrect answers with the exact question and your reasoning.

Independence and contact

Learnora is not an official government curriculum provider and does not claim endorsement by the agencies whose public resources are linked. For questions or corrections, visit our Contact page or About Learnora.
