Chemistry · Grade 8 · Beginner to intermediate

Elements, Compounds and Chemical Reactions

Explore what substances are made of, how atoms combine, and how chemical reactions rearrange particles to form new substances.

Study time: 20–25 minutes · Includes: comparison table, worked examples, practice, quiz and printable worksheet.

What You Will Learn

  • Describe an element as a substance made from one type of atom.
  • Explain that a compound contains two or more elements chemically bonded in fixed proportions.
  • Distinguish pure substances from mixtures.
  • Recognise common evidence that a chemical reaction may have occurred.
  • Explain conservation of mass in a closed system.

1. Atoms and elements

All ordinary matter is made of atoms. An element is a pure substance that contains only one type of atom. Each element has its own chemical symbol.

ElementSymbolExample
HydrogenHPart of water molecules
OxygenOOxygen gas contains O atoms joined in pairs (O₂)
CarbonCFound in graphite and diamond
IronFeUsed in steel production

An element cannot be broken down into simpler substances by ordinary chemical reactions.

2. Compounds and mixtures

A compound forms when atoms of different elements are chemically bonded in a fixed ratio. A mixture contains two or more substances physically combined. Its components are not chemically bonded to one another as a single compound. A mixture can contain elements, compounds or both.

FeatureCompoundMixture
CompositionElements joined in a fixed ratioProportions can vary
PropertiesOften different from the elements that form itComponents usually keep many of their properties
SeparationRequires chemical change to separate into elementsOften separated by physical methods
ExamplePure water (H₂O)Air or salt water
Remember: Water is a compound because hydrogen and oxygen atoms are chemically bonded in a fixed ratio. Salt water is a mixture because salt is dissolved in water and the components can be separated by physical processes such as evaporation.

3. What happens in a chemical reaction?

A chemical reaction changes starting substances, called reactants, into new substances, called products. Atoms are rearranged: bonds break and new bonds form. Atoms are not created or destroyed in an ordinary chemical reaction.

For example, when iron reacts with oxygen and water, rust forms. Rust has properties that differ from those of iron.

4. Evidence of a chemical change

Possible signs include:

  • A lasting colour change that is not simply mixing dyes.
  • Gas bubbles produced without boiling.
  • A new solid (precipitate) forming in a solution.
  • An energy change, such as heat or light being released or absorbed.
  • A new smell caused by a new substance (never deliberately smell unknown chemicals).

One sign alone does not always prove a reaction. For example, bubbles can also come from boiling. Use the full context and safe observations.

5. Conservation of mass

In a chemical reaction, atoms are rearranged but the total number of each type of atom stays the same. Therefore, the total mass of reactants equals the total mass of products when all substances are included and measured in a closed system.

If 12 g of carbon reacts completely with 32 g of oxygen in a closed system, the product has a total mass of 44 g. The mass has not vanished; the atoms have combined in a new arrangement.

If a gas escapes from an open container, the measured mass of what remains may decrease. This does not break the conservation rule: the escaped gas is part of the products and must be included.

6. Physical change or chemical change?

ChangeTypeWhy?
Ice meltingPhysicalWater remains H₂O; only its state changes.
Paper tearingPhysicalThe material's size and shape change, but no new substance is formed.
Iron rustingChemicalIron reacts to form new substances.
Wood burningChemicalCombustion forms new substances such as gases and ash.

7. Practice exercises

  1. Classify oxygen gas (O₂) as an element, compound or mixture. Explain your choice.
  2. Explain why pure water is a compound but salt water is a mixture.
  3. Name two observations that may provide evidence of a chemical reaction.
  4. In a closed system, 8 g of one reactant combines with 5 g of another. What is the total mass of the products?
  5. Explain why melting ice is a physical change.

Choose one answer for each question, then check your feedback.

9. Printable Worksheet

  1. Define element and compound in your own words.
  2. Give one example of a mixture and describe how its components could be separated.
  3. Explain how the formation of rust is different from melting ice.
  4. Describe why a closed system is useful when checking conservation of mass.
  5. A reaction uses 15 g of reactants. What total mass of products should form in a closed system? Explain.

Suggested answer features

  1. An element contains one type of atom; a compound contains different elements chemically bonded in fixed proportions.
  2. Answers may include salt water separated by evaporation or sand and water separated by filtration.
  3. Rusting forms new substances; melting ice changes only the state of water.
  4. A closed system keeps gases and other products from escaping the measurement.
  5. The total product mass is 15 g because mass is conserved.

10. Key Takeaways

  • Elements contain one type of atom; compounds contain different elements chemically bonded in fixed ratios.
  • Mixtures have variable composition and can often be separated physically.
  • Chemical reactions rearrange atoms to form new substances.
  • Mass is conserved when all reactants and products are included in a closed system.
