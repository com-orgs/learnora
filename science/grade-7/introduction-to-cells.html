GRADE 7 · SCIENCE

Introduction to Cells

All living organisms are made of cells. Some organisms consist of one cell, while others are made of many specialised cells. Learn the main ideas of cell theory, explore the structures found in plant and animal cells, and discover how cells are adapted for different jobs.

Learning time: approximately 30 minutes · Beginner level

What You Will Learn

  • Understand what a cell is and describe cell theory.
  • Identify the main structures in animal and plant cells.
  • Explain the basic jobs of common organelles.
  • Compare plant and animal cells.
  • Describe examples of specialised cells.

1. What Is a Cell?

A cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life. It is the smallest unit that can carry out the processes needed for life.

Some living things, such as many bacteria, are single-celled. Plants, animals and many other organisms are multicellular, meaning they are made of many cells.

Think of it this way: Your body contains many different types of cells. They work together to form tissues, organs and organ systems.

2. The Basic Ideas of Cell Theory

Cell theory is a central idea in biology. Its three main statements are:

  1. All living organisms are made of one or more cells.
  2. The cell is the basic unit of structure and function in living things.
  3. All cells come from pre-existing cells.

3. Common Cell Structures

StructureMain function
Cell membraneControls what enters and leaves the cell.
CytoplasmJelly-like material where many cell reactions take place.
NucleusContains genetic material and helps control cell activities in eukaryotic cells.
MitochondriaSite of most aerobic respiration; releases energy usable by the cell.
RibosomesBuild proteins.
Cell wallProvides support and protection in plant cells and some other organisms.
ChloroplastsContain chlorophyll and are the site of photosynthesis in plants and algae.
Large permanent vacuoleStores cell sap and helps maintain pressure in many plant cells.

4. Animal Cells

An animal cell is a eukaryotic cell. It has a cell membrane, cytoplasm, a nucleus, mitochondria and ribosomes. Animal cells do not have a cellulose cell wall or chloroplasts.

Main structures to identify in a typical animal cell:

Cell membrane · Cytoplasm · Nucleus · Mitochondria · Ribosomes

Different animal cell types can have different shapes and numbers of organelles.

5. Plant Cells

Plant cells have many structures found in animal cells, but typical plant cells also have a cellulose cell wall, chloroplasts in photosynthetic tissues and a large permanent vacuole.

Main structures in a typical plant cell:

Cell wall · Cell membrane · Cytoplasm · Nucleus · Chloroplasts · Large permanent vacuole · Mitochondria · Ribosomes

Not every plant cell contains chloroplasts. For example, many root cells are not exposed to light and do not photosynthesise.

7. Unicellular and Multicellular Organisms

A unicellular organism is made of one cell that carries out all the life processes it needs. A multicellular organism is made of many cells that often specialise in different tasks.

  • Examples of unicellular organisms include many bacteria and some protists, such as Amoeba.
  • Humans, trees and most animals are multicellular.

8. Specialised Cells

In multicellular organisms, some cells have structures that help them perform particular jobs.

Cell typeAdaptationFunction
Red blood cellContains haemoglobin and has a shape that helps exchange gasesTransports oxygen around the body
Nerve cell (neurone)Has a long fibre and branched connectionsCarries electrical signals
Root hair cellHas a long extension that increases surface areaAbsorbs water and mineral ions from soil
Muscle cellContains contractile proteinsContracts to produce movement
Sperm cellHas a tail and many mitochondria in its midpieceMoves towards and fertilises an egg cell

9. Looking at Cells with a Microscope

Microscopes make small structures appear larger so they can be observed. A simple school light microscope uses lenses and light.

  1. Place a prepared slide on the stage and secure it.
  2. Start with the lowest-power objective lens.
  3. Use the focus controls carefully to make the image clear.
  4. Adjust the light so the specimen can be seen.
  5. Move to a higher magnification only when needed, and refocus carefully.
Magnification and size are different: Magnification tells how many times larger an image appears. It does not mean the actual specimen has become bigger.

10. Common Mistakes to Avoid

Assuming all plant cells have chloroplasts: Only photosynthetic plant cells typically contain chloroplasts.
Thinking plant cells do not have mitochondria: Plant cells carry out respiration and contain mitochondria.
Mixing cell wall and cell membrane: The wall provides support; the membrane controls movement into and out of the cell.
Thinking every cell has a nucleus: Mature mammalian red blood cells, for example, do not have a nucleus.

  3. What is the main function of the cell membrane?
  4. Which structure contains chlorophyll and carries out photosynthesis?
  5. Why do root hair cells have a long extension?
  6. What is one difference between a unicellular and a multicellular organism?
  7. Which specialised cell carries electrical signals?
Show practice answers and explanations

  2. Examples include cell membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus, mitochondria and ribosomes.
  3. It controls what enters and leaves the cell.
  4. The chloroplast.
  5. It increases surface area for absorbing water and mineral ions.
  6. A unicellular organism has one cell; a multicellular organism has many cells.
  7. A nerve cell, or neurone.

14. Lesson Summary

  • Cells are the basic units of life.
  • Cell theory explains that living organisms are made of cells and new cells come from existing cells.
  • Plant and animal cells share many structures, including a cell membrane, cytoplasm and mitochondria.
  • Typical plant cells also have a cellulose cell wall and a large permanent vacuole; photosynthetic cells have chloroplasts.
  • Specialised cells have adaptations that help them perform particular functions.
