How to Use Learnora

Learnora is a free learning website for Grades 6–8. Every lesson takes about 25–35 minutes and ends with a quiz and a printable worksheet. There is no account to create, no payment and no tracking.

Jump to: Start here · How a lesson works · The 25-minute method · For parents · For teachers · Study plans · Finding things · Questions

Start here in 2 minutes

1. Pick a subject

Choose a subject from the subjects on the homepage: Mathematics, English, Science, Computer Science, AI Literacy or Financial Literacy.

2. Pick your grade

Open your grade page — for example Grade 7 Mathematics — and choose a topic you find hard or interesting.

3. Start the lesson

Read the learning goals first, then work through the lesson. Try a worked example yourself before reading its solution.

Our promise: every lesson ends with a quiz and a printable worksheet, so you can check what you learned and practise again tomorrow.

How a lesson works

Every Learnora lesson follows the same shape, so you always know what comes next:

  1. Learning goals — what you will be able to do by the end of the lesson.
  2. Explanations — short sections that build the idea one step at a time.
  3. Worked examples — problems solved step by step, with a check at the end.
  4. Common mistakes — the errors learners make most often, with the corrected version.
  5. Independent practice — questions to try on paper before opening the answers.
  6. Quiz — five questions with instant feedback and explanations.
  7. Random quiz — ten fresh questions, different every time, with worked solutions. A teacher can set a seed so the whole class gets the same ten questions.
  8. Printable worksheet — new questions with an answer key for parents and teachers.
  9. Summary and sources — the key ideas in a few lines, and where to read more.
Try one now: How to Solve Two-Step Equations (Mathematics, Grade 7) or Parts of Speech (English, Grades 6–8).

The 25-minute method for students

  1. Read the goals (2 minutes). Know what you are aiming to learn.
  2. Cover and try (10 minutes). Read a worked example, cover its solution, and try it yourself on paper.
  3. Do the practice (8 minutes). Answer the practice questions before opening the answers. If you get one wrong, read the explanation and re-read the matching section.
  4. Take the quiz (5 minutes). Then try the random quiz for a fresh set of questions.
  5. Print the worksheet for tomorrow. Do it again the next day without looking at the answers first — trying again from memory is how learning sticks.

When you get a question wrong: read the worked solution, find the step where you went wrong, and try a similar question. A wrong answer is useful — it shows exactly what to practise next.

Keep the 25 minutes honest: study with the Study Timer → — 25 minutes of study, then a 5-minute break.

For parents

  • Help without doing the work. Check the answer key together and ask your child to explain their working — explaining is the best test of understanding.
  • Use the worksheet as homework. Print it, set a time, and mark it with the answer key.
  • Twenty minutes a day beats two hours once a week. Short, regular practice works better than cramming.
  • Praise effort, not just scores. A child who retries a hard question is learning more than one who only answers easy ones.

What Learnora does and does not do: the site is free, needs no account, shows no advertisements, and collects no personal data from children. It is supplementary practice, not a replacement for school or a teacher.

For teachers

  • In class: project a worked example, work through it together, then set the practice questions as classwork.
  • As homework: set the printable worksheet; the answer key is included for marking.
  • Same quiz for the whole class: in the random quiz section, type a seed such as 7B, press “Use this seed”, and every student who enters the same seed gets the same ten questions.
  • Citing Learnora: link to the lesson page. Lessons are curriculum-neutral supplementary practice — check the examples against your local curriculum before using them as a lesson sequence.

Honest limits: Learnora lessons are written and checked by people, with sources listed on each lesson, but they are not a textbook and not a substitute for a teacher. Every answer is independently checked; if you find an error, please tell us through the contact page.

Study plans

Twenty minutes a day: one lesson section, five practice questions, and the five-question quiz. Print the worksheet and finish it the next day.

A weekly Mathematics plan (Grade 7 example)

  • Monday: fractions and decimals in problem solving.
  • Tuesday: two-step equations.
  • Wednesday: ratios and proportional relationships.
  • Thursday: practice — redo the week's worksheets without the answer keys.
  • Friday: quiz day — take the fixed quiz and a random quiz for each topic.

Tick each day off when it is done. If a day is missed, do not double up the next day — just continue the plan.

Finding things

Use the subjects on the homepage or the search box on the resources section to find a topic. Subjects covered are Mathematics, English, Science, Computer Science, AI Literacy and Financial Literacy.

Can’t find a topic? Tell us through the contact page — we add topics that learners ask for. New topics are built as full lessons, never as empty pages.

Frequently asked questions

Is Learnora free?

Yes. Every lesson, quiz and worksheet is free, with no account and no payment.

Do I need an account?

No. There are no accounts, no logins and no progress stored on a server. The random quiz keeps your current set only in your own browser until you close the tab.

Can I print the worksheets?

Yes. Every lesson has a printable worksheet with an answer key. Use the print button on the lesson page.

Is the maths correct?

Every answer is independently checked before a lesson is published, and sources are listed on each lesson. If you find a mistake, please report it on the contact page.

Who is Learnora for?

Learners in Grades 6–8, their parents and their teachers. Lessons are curriculum-neutral, so they work alongside any local curriculum as extra practice.

Does the site work for screen readers and keyboards?

Lessons use labelled quiz questions, keyboard-operable controls and print-friendly worksheets. If you find an accessibility problem, please tell us on the contact page.

A note on AI

Our AI Literacy lessons explain how AI works and how to check AI answers. Learnora lessons themselves are written and checked by people, with sources listed — AI tools are not used to write lesson content. If you use an AI tool while studying, check its answers against a trusted source and follow your teacher’s rules.
