Verb Tenses and Subject–Verb Agreement

Verbs tell what happens and when it happens. Correct verb forms help readers understand time, while subject–verb agreement keeps sentences grammatically consistent.

Learning time: approximately 25–30 minutes · Curriculum-neutral learning support

What You Will Learn

  • Choose present, past and future verb forms to match the time of an event.
  • Form simple tenses with regular and common irregular verbs.
  • Make verbs agree with singular and plural subjects.
  • Recognise common agreement traps involving words between the subject and verb.
  • Keep verb tense consistent unless the time changes.

1. What tense tells us

Verb tense places an action or state in time. The simple present describes habits, facts and regular events; the simple past describes completed past events; the simple future describes events expected later.

TenseExampleUse
PresentShe walks to school.Habit or general fact
PastShe walked yesterday.Completed past action
FutureShe will walk tomorrow.Expected future action

2. Regular and irregular verbs

Many regular verbs form the past tense by adding -ed: talk → talked, clean → cleaned. Some verbs are irregular and change form: go → went, write → wrote, take → took, see → saw.

Examples: Today I write a note. Yesterday I wrote a note. Tomorrow I will write a note.

Learn common irregular forms and check unfamiliar verbs in a reliable dictionary.

3. Subject–verb agreement

A verb must agree with its subject in number. In the simple present, a singular third-person subject usually takes a verb ending in -s or -es: “The bird sings.” A plural subject uses the base form: “The birds sing.”

Singular: The player runs. Plural: The players run. Be verb: She is ready; they are ready.

4. Find the real subject

Words between the subject and verb do not usually change the agreement. Find the main subject, not the nearest noun.

Incorrect: The box of pencils are on the desk.
Correct: The box of pencils is on the desk. The subject is box, which is singular.

With and, two subjects usually form a plural subject: “Mina and Ali are ready.” With or or nor, the verb generally agrees with the nearer subject in standard school grammar: “Either the teacher or the students are presenting.”

5. Keep tense consistent

Use the same tense for actions that happen in the same time frame. Change tense when the time changes, not randomly.

Inconsistent: Yesterday we visited the museum and see the fossils.
Consistent: Yesterday we visited the museum and saw the fossils.

Time words such as yesterday, now, every day, last week and tomorrow help you choose the correct tense.

Worked Examples

  1. Present: The class studies English every day.
  2. Past: The class studied English yesterday.
  3. Future: The class will study English tomorrow.
  4. Agreement: The list of names is on the table. The subject is list, not names.

Independent Practice

Try each question before revealing the answers.

  1. Change to past tense: They visit the museum.
  2. Change to future tense: I finish my homework.
  3. Choose: The dog (bark / barks) at strangers.
  4. Choose: The players (is / are) ready.
  5. Correct: The basket of apples are heavy.
  6. Correct the tense: Last night we eat dinner and watched a film.
Show practice answers and explanations
  1. They visited the museum.
  2. I will finish my homework.
  3. barks — dog is singular.
  4. are — players is plural.
  5. The basket of apples is heavy. The subject is basket.
  6. Last night we ate dinner and watched a film.

Lesson Summary

  • Verb tense places an action or state in time; keep tense consistent unless the meaning changes.
  • Regular verbs usually form the past with -ed, while irregular verbs have different forms.
  • A verb must agree with its subject in number and person.
  • Find the true subject before choosing a verb, especially when phrases come between them.
  • Proofread for tense shifts and subject–verb agreement.
