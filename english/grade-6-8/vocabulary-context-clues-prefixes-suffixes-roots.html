Vocabulary Skills: Context Clues, Prefixes, Suffixes and Roots

You do not need to stop at every unfamiliar word. Readers can use the surrounding sentence, familiar word parts and reliable reference tools to work out a likely meaning.

Study time: 25–30 minutes · Includes: original examples, independent practice, five-question quiz and printable worksheet.

What You Will Learn

  • Use context clues to make a sensible first guess about an unfamiliar word.
  • Recognise common prefixes, suffixes and root words.
  • Use word families to connect spelling, meaning and grammar.
  • Check a possible meaning with a dictionary and reread the sentence.
  • Choose precise vocabulary without changing the intended meaning.

1. Use context clues

Context means the words and ideas surrounding a word. A writer may explain an unfamiliar word, give an example, show a contrast or describe the result of an action.

Clue typeWhat to look forExample
Definition or explanationThe sentence explains the word directly.A nocturnal animal is active at night.
ExampleSpecific examples show the category.Arid places, such as dry deserts, receive very little rainfall.
ContrastWords such as unlike, but or whereas show a difference.Unlike her timid brother, Amina was confident speaking to a crowd.
Cause or resultThe outcome helps explain the word.The path was slippery, so we walked cautiously.
RestatementThe writer repeats an idea in simpler words.The room was immaculate; not a speck of dust could be seen.
Worked Example A: contrast clue

“The first route was arduous, but the second was short and easy.” The word but signals a contrast. If the second route was easy, the first was probably difficult or tiring. So arduous means requiring great effort.

Important: Context clues give you a likely meaning, not always the exact dictionary definition. Keep more than one possibility in mind until the sentence or a reference source confirms it.

2. Understand prefixes

A prefix is a word part added to the beginning of a base word. It changes or adds to the word's meaning.

PrefixCommon meaningExample
un-not; opposite ofunfair = not fair
re-again; backrewrite = write again
pre-beforepreview = view beforehand
mis-wrongly; badlymisread = read incorrectly
dis-not; opposite; removaldisagree = have a different opinion
inter-between; amonginternational = between nations
Worked Example B: build a word

Start with understand. Add the prefix mis-: misunderstand. The new word means to understand something incorrectly.

3. Understand suffixes

A suffix is a word part added to the end of a base word. It can change the meaning or the grammatical job of the word.

SuffixCommon useExample
-fulfull ofcareful = showing care
-lesswithoutcareless = without enough care
-nessforms a noun for a state or qualitykindness = the quality of being kind
-era person or thing that does an actionteacher = a person who teaches
-ablecan be; suitable forreadable = able to be read easily
-lyoften forms an adverbcarefully = in a careful way
Worked Example C: change the word's job

Kind is an adjective. Adding -ness creates kindness, a noun naming the quality. The spelling may change when a suffix is added, so check the finished word.

4. Find roots and word families

A root carries a core meaning. Some roots come from Latin or Greek and appear in many English words. A word family contains related forms built from the same base or root.

RootCore meaningRelated words
telefar; distanttelephone, television, telescope
biolifebiology, biography, biodegradable
portcarrytransport, portable, import
spectlook; seeinspect, spectator, spectacles
dictsay; speakdictate, predict, dictionary

Word parts are clues, not a guarantee. Meanings can develop over time, and a familiar-looking part may not have its usual meaning in every word.

5. Use a dictionary wisely

  1. Read the full sentence and the sentence before or after it.
  2. Make a possible meaning using context clues.
  3. Separate any prefix, root or suffix you recognise.
  4. Look up the word and check its pronunciation, part of speech and the definition that fits this sentence.
  5. Reread the original sentence with the definition. Does it make sense?

Many words have more than one meaning. For example, bright can describe strong light, a vivid colour or a clever person. Choose the meaning that fits the context.

6. Build precise vocabulary

When you learn a new word, record its meaning, part of speech, a useful example and any common word parts. Try using it in a sentence of your own. Avoid choosing a complicated word if it does not express your meaning accurately.

Compare: “The runner moved fast” is clear. “The runner moved swiftly” may be more precise in some contexts, but both are correct. A thesaurus can suggest alternatives; always check that the replacement fits the sentence.

7. Independent Practice

Try these questions before checking the quiz. Write your reasoning, not just the answer.

  1. In “The glass was fragile, so it broke when it fell,” what does fragile probably mean? Which clue helped?
  2. Break preview into its parts. What does the prefix suggest?
  3. What suffix changes kind into the noun kindness?
  4. Use the root tele to explain the basic idea shared by telephone and telescope.
  5. Write one sentence using carefully and identify its part of speech.

8. Test Your Understanding

Choose one answer for each question, then select Check Answers. Feedback explains the reasoning.

1. In “The arid land received almost no rain,” what does arid mean?

2. What does the prefix re- usually mean in the word rewrite?

3. Which word contains a suffix that forms a noun naming a quality?

4. What core meaning does the root bio carry?

5. What is the best final step after guessing a word's meaning from context?

9. Printable Worksheet

Complete the tasks. Use the space in your notebook for full sentences.

  1. Read: “The path was treacherous after the storm; loose stones made every step risky.” Write a likely meaning for treacherous and identify the clue.
  2. Explain the meaning of the prefix mis- and give two examples.
  3. Use -less to make a word from hope. Explain its meaning.
  4. Write two words containing the root spect and explain their connection to looking or seeing.
  5. Choose one unfamiliar word from your reading. Record its context, your first guess, a dictionary definition and a sentence of your own.

Suggested Answer Features

  1. Treacherous means dangerous or unsafe; “loose stones” and “every step risky” are context clues.
  2. Mis- means wrongly or badly; examples include misread and misunderstand.
  3. Hopeless means without hope.
  4. Inspect means look at carefully; a spectator is someone who watches.
  5. Answers will vary. The dictionary meaning should fit the sentence and the learner's own sentence should use the word accurately.

10. Lesson Summary

  • Read around an unfamiliar word and use definition, example, contrast, cause or restatement clues.
  • Prefixes appear at the beginning of words; suffixes appear at the end.
  • Roots carry core meanings and can connect words in a family.
  • Use a dictionary to check meaning, pronunciation and part of speech.
  • Reread the sentence and choose vocabulary that communicates the intended idea precisely.
