GRADES 6–8 · ENGLISH

Parts of Speech

Every word has a job in a sentence. Parts of speech describe how words work: they name people and things, show actions, describe details and connect ideas. Learn the eight main traditional parts of speech with examples.

Learning time: approximately 30 minutes · Beginner level

What You Will Learn

  • Identify nouns and pronouns.
  • Recognise verbs and helping verbs.
  • Use adjectives and adverbs to add detail.
  • Understand prepositions, conjunctions and interjections.
  • Identify how a word functions in a sentence.

1. Nouns: Naming Words

A noun names a person, place, thing or idea.

  • Person: teacher, child, doctor.
  • Place: school, city, library.
  • Thing: pencil, phone, bicycle.
  • Idea: courage, kindness, freedom.
Sentence: The student placed a book on the desk. “Student”, “book” and “desk” are nouns.

2. Pronouns: Words That Replace Nouns

A pronoun can take the place of a noun to avoid repetition.

Common examples include I, you, he, she, it, we, they, me, him, her, us and them.

Example: Sara is helpful. She helps her classmates. The pronoun “she” replaces “Sara”.

Make sure a pronoun clearly refers to the correct person or thing.

3. Verbs: Actions and States

A verb tells what someone or something does, or describes a state of being.

  • Action verbs: run, write, think, build.
  • Being verbs: am, is, are, was, were.
  • Helping verbs: has, have, will, can, should.
Example: The children are playing outside. “Are” helps the main verb “playing”.

4. Adjectives: Words That Describe Nouns

An adjective gives information about a noun or pronoun. It can describe size, colour, number, shape, quality or feeling.

Example: The bright yellow kite flew high. “Bright” and “yellow” describe the kite.

5. Adverbs: Words That Add Information

An adverb can describe a verb, an adjective or another adverb. It often tells how, when, where or how often something happens.

  • How: carefully, quickly.
  • When: yesterday, soon.
  • Where: outside, nearby.
  • How often: always, sometimes.
Example: The runner moved quickly. “Quickly” describes how the runner moved.
Remember: Not every adverb ends in “-ly”, and not every word ending in “-ly” is an adverb.

6. Prepositions: Showing Relationships

A preposition shows a relationship between a noun or pronoun and another word. It often tells where or when something is.

Examples: in, on, under, beside, between, before, after, during and through.

Example: The keys are under the table. “Under” shows the relationship between the keys and the table.

7. Conjunctions: Connecting Words and Ideas

A conjunction joins words, phrases or clauses.

  • Coordinating conjunctions: for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so.
  • Common subordinating conjunctions: because, although, when, if, while.
Example: I wanted to play, but it started raining. “But” connects two ideas.

8. Interjections: Expressing Feelings

An interjection is a word or short expression that shows a sudden feeling or reaction.

Examples: Wow! Ouch! Hurray! Oh! Alas!

Example: Wow! That science experiment worked. “Wow” expresses surprise or excitement.

10. One Sentence, Many Jobs

Read this sentence: “Wow! The clever student quickly placed her bag beside the chair because it was raining.”

  • Wow! — interjection.
  • student, bag, chair — nouns.
  • clever — adjective.
  • quickly — adverb.
  • placed, was raining — verbs.
  • her, it — pronouns or determiners depending on grammatical framework and use; here “her” functions as a possessive determiner before “bag”, while “it” is a pronoun.
  • beside — preposition.
  • because — conjunction.

Some grammar books classify possessive words such as “her” as determiners rather than pronouns when they come before a noun. Always follow the terminology used by your teacher or curriculum.

11. Common Mistakes to Avoid

Calling every describing word an adjective: Adjectives describe nouns or pronouns; adverbs can describe verbs, adjectives or other adverbs.
Assuming every verb is an action: “Is”, “are” and “was” can be verbs too.
Confusing a preposition and conjunction: A preposition shows a relationship; a conjunction connects words or clauses.
Identifying words without context: A word can perform different jobs in different sentences.

12. Practice Exercises

Identify the part of speech of the bold word in each sentence.

  1. The **teacher** explained the lesson.
  2. **They** visited the museum.
  3. The baby **laughed** loudly.
  4. It was a **wonderful** day.
  5. The dog barked **loudly**.
  6. The shoes are **under** the bed.
  7. I stayed home **because** it rained.
  8. **Hurray!** Our team won.

  2. Pronoun.

15. Lesson Summary

  • Nouns name people, places, things and ideas.
  • Pronouns replace nouns.
  • Verbs show actions or states.
  • Adjectives describe nouns and pronouns; adverbs add information about verbs, adjectives and adverbs.
  • Prepositions show relationships; conjunctions connect; interjections express reactions.
  • Always use the sentence context to identify a word's job.
